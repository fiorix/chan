# Every click to a terminal tab sends focus-in, focus-out, focus-in

Status: carried into v0.106.0 at the v0.105.0 GA on 2026-10-11, still raised for a decision.

Record before the release: raised for v0.105.0 by the owner's word of 2026-10-09, from the v0.104.0 release report's follow-ups; written down, not designed, not accepted for build.

## What was seen

A measured observation whose cause is not read. Each click that activates a terminal tab makes the page send its `focus {"focused": true}` message twice, 2 to 42 ms apart in the subject blocks of the two captures read below, and a program that has enabled focus reporting (DECSET 1004) receives focus-in, focus-out, focus-in (`CSI I`, `CSI O`, `CSI I`) where one focus-in is expected, so it runs its focus-lost and focus-gained handling one extra time per activation. The record is `dev/v0104-team/reviews/review-Review104-item6-range-1.md`, section "Observations outside the range, for the lead", entry X2, headed "existing, both backends": measured in the archived xterm.js capture and in the ghostty-web captures of the v0.104.0 repair, the three reports within 1 to 12 ms with the `focus` message sent twice, and "The cause is not read (the `focused` effect re-runs on `tabFocusPulse`; what takes the focus between the two is not established)".

Both captures were read again for this item, block by block. Under xterm.js: `../chan-dev/releases/v0103-team/evidence/Desktop103/terminal-dot/runs/capture-04/bus/xterm-standin-report.summary.txt` (a sibling checkout of the repository), four `click:DotSubject` blocks, each with `CSI I`, `CSI O`, `CSI I` sent to the clicked terminal within 1 ms by the summary's stamps and the `focus` message twice; that run's guest script (`payload/guest.sh` in the same run directory) names puppeteer's Chrome 154.0.8037.92 on Linux. Under ghostty-web: `dev/v0104-team/evidence/Frontend104/item6/capture-after.log`, twelve `click:DotSubject` blocks over three stand-ins on Chrome for Testing 155 headless (`dev/v0104-team/reports/validation-Frontend104-item6.md`, section "Acceptance", point 5): the eight blocks of the two stand-ins that turn reporting on each show the three reports within 12 ms, and all twelve blocks, the four of the stand-in with reporting off among them, show the `focus` message twice. In both files, where reporting is on, a click away from the subject sends it one `CSI O`. So the doubled `focus` message does not depend on the mode, and the three reports are what a program with the mode on sees of it.

Under ghostty-web a program sees this only since v0.104.0, which made that backend send the reports: the closed item records "on both backends every click to a terminal tab sends focus-in, focus-out, focus-in within milliseconds", "a shape present in the archived v0103 xterm capture and in this range's captures alike" (`team/roadmap/done/ghostty-web-sends-no-focus-report.md`, section "Landed 2026-10-08"), and the review says of it "Not this range's doing: with the repair the ghostty backend now shows what xterm.js users already had". The release report lists it under "Observed without a repair" and in its Follow-ups (`team/release/release-v0.104.0.md`).

The source the review points at, read at the v0.104.0 commit and not traced further: in `web/packages/workspace-app/src/components/TerminalTab.svelte` the effect at lines 524 to 549 runs when the tab is focused and again when the global tab-focus pulse changes, and each run calls `sendFocusState()` and then, in a microtask, `focusTerminal()`; the effect that starts at line 580 blurs the terminal and calls `sendFocusState()` when the tab stops being focused.

Not established: the cause, that is, what takes the focus away between the two focus-ins and why the `focus` message goes out twice; whether an activation by keyboard chord, by a command or by a pane focus change does the same, since both captures drive clicks on the tab strip; the behaviour on WebKitGTK, WKWebView and WebView2; any misbehaviour of a real program, since the record says only that a program's handlers run one extra time; whether the second `focus` message costs the server anything.

## Desired contract

The item asks for the cause and then a decision. The choices: one activation of a terminal tab sends one `focus` message and, with reporting on, one `CSI I`; or the present sequence stands and is recorded as accepted, with its cause. The release report words the question as whether the sequence "needs a change".

## What to do

Read the cause first: trace in `TerminalTab.svelte` what runs between the first focus-in and the focus-out on a tab click (the two focus effects, the tab-focus pulse, the pointer event on the tab strip, and what `focusTerminal` and `blur` do under each backend). Confirm the reading in a guest's Chrome with the existing capture driver or a constructed page, beside an activation that the reading predicts sends one report. Say whether keyboard and command activations share it, measured or not. Report the cause to the lead with the two choices before any change.

## Boundaries

`web/packages/workspace-app/src/components/TerminalTab.svelte`, the tab strip component where its pointer handling takes part, and their tests. Not changed: the reports themselves and their mode gate under either backend, the ghostty-web rule that a focus move inside the terminal sends nothing, the unseen-output dot's reading of focus, and the renderer recovery that the same focus effects run.

## Acceptance

1. The cause written down with its source lines and a run that shows it: what moves focus away and back on a tab click, on which backend and engine.
2. Whether an activation by keyboard chord and by command sends the same sequence: measured, or said to be unmeasured.
3. The decision recorded here.
4. If the decision is a change: a pin, red first on its own assertion, that one activation sends one `focus` message and, with reporting on, one `CSI I`, under both backends; a capture read again with one focus-in per click; a click away still sends one `CSI O`; `make web-check` green at the commit in the owning guest.

## The cause and the lead's decision 2026-10-13

Acceptance 1 and 2 (the frontend seat, `dev/v0106-team/tasks/task-Frontend106-Lead106-23.md`; `evidence/Frontend106/ftrip-*`). The cause, at source: the tab strip is a focusable `div` (`Pane.svelte` 1462 to 1503) whose mousedown handler selects the tab and bumps the tab-focus pulse (1467 to 1472; `selectTabInPane` bumps it too, `state/tabs.svelte.ts` 5400 to 5409), the two bumps coalescing into one run of the focused effect (`TerminalTab.svelte` 540 to 566), which sends `focus: true` and queues `focusTerminal()`; the browser's mousedown default action then focuses the strip's own `tabindex=0` element, taking DOM focus from the terminal just focused; the mouseup handler (1473 to 1485) bumps the pulse again, so the same effect sends a second `focus: true` and refocuses the terminal; the strip's comment names the default-action race and keeps the default action for drag start. Under xterm.js the focus target is the helper textarea, whose blur and focus make `CSI O` and `CSI I` through the library's own handler; under ghostty-web the host element's listeners report the crossing because the strip lies outside the host. The keyboard chord and the command both go through `selectTabAtIndexInActivePane` (`tabs.svelte.ts` 3671 to 3677), which selects and bumps once with no default action and no second pulse. Confirmed in the guest's Chrome for Testing 155 against the real application with two terminal tabs per backend and reporting enabled in the source terminal alone: a strip click to the subject sends `CSI I`, `CSI O`, `CSI I` and two `focus: true` frames under both backends (3.0 ms apart under xterm.js, 1.9 ms under ghostty-web); a click away `CSI O` and one `focus: false`; the chord and the command one `CSI I` and one `focus: true` each. Event-order observations, not performance bounds; no native WebView.

Acceptance 3, the decision: one activation, one report. A click that activates a terminal tab gets the contract the chord and the command already have: one `focus` message and, with reporting on, one `CSI I`. The design, a short note by the frontend seat for the review seat's read before code: which of the two shapes the reading supports (the strip's mousedown default action prevented when the pressed tab's terminal is the one being activated, with drag start kept by the pointer events rather than the default focus; or the mouseup pulse skipped when the first pulse's focus already landed on the terminal), inside the item's boundaries, with acceptance 4's pins (one activation, one message and one report under both backends, red first; a click away still one `CSI O`; the capture read again with one focus-in per click; the whole web gate the integration guest's at the landing by the round's rule).

## The design read 2026-10-13

Review106 (`dev/v0106-team/reviews/review-Review106-ftrip-design-1.md`) read the frontend seat's note (`reports/design-Frontend106-ftrip.md`) against the strip, the terminal component and the tab state: the two shapes the lead named are rightly rejected (preventing the strip's mousedown default kills native drag start in WebKit and Firefox, which the strip's comment records; the release pulse is what returns focus to a file editor, and by the release the default action has already moved focus to the strip), and the seat's third shape, a mouseup-owned terminal click (the press remembered, the matching release selecting once through `selectTabInPane`, a drag start cancelling the press and selecting the dragged source, the pane's bubbling press skipped for the hold), is sound by source and keeps every "not changed" of the boundaries because `TerminalTab.svelte` stays as it is: one run of the focused effect per click after the default focus has landed, so one `focus: true` and one `CSI I`; a click away still one `CSI O` at the press and one `focus: false` at the release. The cost to a person, recorded and accepted by the lead: a terminal tab becomes the visible tab on the release (a file tab still on the press), a held press shows nothing until the release, a press and drag selects at the drag start, a press that slides off and releases elsewhere no longer activates; typing is unchanged, since today the browser takes the press-time focus back inside the same click and the terminal holds the keyboard only from the release. The one shape that would keep the visual switch at the press has to hold the focused effect back in `TerminalTab.svelte`, which the boundaries keep unchanged; it is not taken by default and is the owner's to ask for.

Five amendments, all ruled in: every ending of a press named with its own case and mutant (the matching release; an outside release through a document-level mouseup removed on destroy; window blur while held; the pressed tab removed or the pane turning stale while held; a `contextmenu` during the press, whose handler already selects, so the release over the open menu must not select again; a non-primary press kept on today's path, said so; no way for the token to outlive its press uncovered); the pane deferral pinned (a press on a terminal tab in an inactive pane whose current tab is another terminal sends that third terminal nothing, the active pane moving at the release) with the skip removed as its mutant, `onPaneBodyMouseDown` kept for the transaction grab; the drag start as the risky edge: a real drag arm in the Chrome driver (an inactive terminal tab dragged to a new place ends selected with one `focus: true` and one `CSI I`), and one native observation under WebKitGTK that a terminal tab's drag still starts and completes, by the desktop seat's observation scripts if the round's time allows, else the gap named here for the owner's hand check; acceptance 4's pin as something the gate runs: the `focus` message half in a mounted test (two sends per click today, one after, under both backends, red first), the `CSI I` half as a check in the browser suite with a program that records its PTY input (as the capture's did), or, if the suite cannot host one, the driver's saved runs standing for acceptance 4 by the lead's ruling, stated; and a click on the already-active tab said and pinned (by source after the change: `CSI O` at the press, one `focus: true` and one `CSI I` at the release, no `focus: false` between; two of each today). The mutants grow with the endings and the deferral. The whole web gate is the integration guest's at the landing.

## The range reviewed and landed 2026-10-13

The review seat read the commit (its verdict of 13:52Z in the round's bus, `reviews/review-Review106-ftrip-range-1.md`) and it is landed. Built as the design read and the lead's rulings ask: a primary press on a terminal tab is remembered and selects nothing; the matching release selects once; other buttons and file tabs keep the press path; the terminal's own component is unchanged. One activation is one `focus: true` and one `CSI I` on both backends, for the click, the chord, the command and the drag, read by the review at the exact commit in Chrome 155 with the new browser check, whose recorder is a real program in the tab's shell in raw mode with reporting switched on by its own sequence. The check is green alone and between its neighbours and red over the base's component on the first click ("1b5b491b5b4f1b5b49" where "1b5b49" is expected). The four modules 126 of 126; the base's component under the tip's tests 15 failed, each on its own assertion; the seat's nineteen mutants each red in the test it names. Every way a press can end has its case and its mutant (the matching release, a release elsewhere, blur, a cancelled pointer, a removed tab, a stale pane, the context menu, a drag's start and end, the listener's removal), and the review found no way for a press to outlive its ending. In Chrome a native drag sends `dragstart` before `pointercancel`, so a cancelled drag still selects its source at its end (measured by the review on a constructed page). The cause of the base's second frame under a drag is not established and is not needed: the contract is an outcome and it is measured.

Two low findings, both ruled by the lead as built in one test-only commit. The browser check decides "one report, not three" by a fixed 250 ms pause after the first byte, so a second report later than that on a loaded guest would pass unseen (a wrong green, never a wrong red; the base's two frames were 3 ms apart): each action first requires that nothing arrived since the previous reading, with one last comparison before the tabs close. And two things have no pin: the cancelled-pointer and blur listeners are not shown removed when a pane is destroyed, and the activity mark's clearing, which moved from the press to the release with the selection, is read by no test. Three more surviving mutants duplicate another guard and stay as they are.

The cost accepted with the decision is unchanged: a terminal tab shows on the release, not on the press. For the native read, which the desktop seat's tab-drag observation carries: the engine's order of `pointercancel` and `dragstart`. An engine that cancels the pointer first, or blurs the window at a drag's start, would leave a cancelled drag's tab unselected; the drag itself would be unharmed. Not observed: any native engine, Firefox.

## The two low findings built and landed; three observations held 2026-10-13

The hygiene seat's test-only commit, read by the review seat (`reviews/review-Review106-ftrip-offsets-1.md` in the round's bus) and landed on `v0106/int` as `2b021ea26`: before every action the browser check compares the cumulative PTY bytes and the focus-frame count with the previous action's reading and fails naming both actions if either grew; after the action it waits until the new bytes and frames each reach what the action is expected to send, bounded at fifteen seconds, then compares the exact slice; after the last action it holds 500 ms and compares once more. The pane's destroy row checks that the `pointercancel` and `blur` listeners are registered and removed as the `mouseup` one is; a release row checks that the activity mark is cleared at the matching release, not at the press and not a turn later, reading the pane's own mark. Evidence: a product mutant in the hygiene seat's guest (a second `focus: true` frame 400 ms after the first, armed at `dragstart`) passes the check of the section above and is red on this one ("late report before tab close", the frame 400.2 ms after the first); the corrected check ten times alone on the clean product, ten green; the review constructed a two-chunk report, held until the second chunk; bytes of the previous action are red at the late comparison, each half held by its own mutant; one arrangement passes and takes two defects at once; the two pane rows each red under their mutant alone. The review's own correction: with the pane's clearing gone the terminal tab's focus effect clears the mark one settle later, so the row holds the order rather than the only clearing.

Three observations of that review, held and not built this round (each improves what a red says or records; none changes what the check detects): the wait's timeout sentence prints the whole record and not what was wanted; a report of one action that arrives while the next is performed is red under the next action's name; a redial of the terminal's socket inside the check would be a red the frame record could not tell from a duplicate (the socket's request id kept in the frame would show it; the read deadline is 45 s against a check of about ten seconds a backend). Not observed: the check in a browser at the landed commit by the review (the seat's runs are at its own tip); any engine but Chrome.
