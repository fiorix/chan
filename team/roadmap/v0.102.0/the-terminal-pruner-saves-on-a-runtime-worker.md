# The terminal pruner saves the window registry under the chan home on a runtime worker

Status: accepted by the owner on 2026-09-27 for a later version than v0.101.0, so it is held under v0.102.0; raised during v0.101.0 on 2026-09-27 by the independent review of the Files watch bound ([a-quit-can-hang-on-a-standalone-files-watch](../done/a-quit-can-hang-on-a-standalone-files-watch.md); `dev/v0101-team/reviews/review-Services-10.md` in the development tree, finding 4), which read the code at `24731ad21`; read again in code at `b1ef073ae`, where the path holds. The hang on a chan home that stopped answering is inferred and not reproduced. On 2026-10-05 the pruner's tick was moved off the runtime worker; the row stays at build for the restart manifest's writes at a devserver's quit.

## Owner ruling

Accepted on 2026-09-27 for a later version, as the lead recommended: the owner accepted in one answer every recommendation the lead had put to them that day, and for this item the recommendation was a later version. It is not part of v0.101.0.

## What was seen

The shared terminal tenant's pruner is a runtime task with a minute tick that reaps exited sessions (`spawn_pruner`, `crates/chan-library/src/terminal_sessions.rs:3318-3336`). Reaping a detached session whose shell exited fires the tenant's window reaper (`reap_exited`, `:3216-3254`, the call at `:3248-3250`), which removes the standalone terminal's row from the window registry (`crates/chan-library/src/host.rs:1711-1729`), and the removal saves the registry inline, an atomic write of the whole store (`remove_terminal`, `crates/chan-library/src/windows.rs:560-579`; `save_best_effort`, `:652-681`). The store is a file under the chan home: `windows.json` beside the devserver's configuration (`crates/chan-server/src/devserver.rs:2112-2119`, under `devserver_state_dir`, `:309-321`), and the same file under the desktop's chan home (the comment at `desktop/src-tauri/src/embedded.rs:101`).

So with the chan home on a mount that stopped answering, a tick can block inside that save on a runtime worker. At a quit, the task owner gives the pruner its five-second grace and then aborts it, but an abort takes effect only at the task's next yield, as the review read tokio, so it cannot interrupt a tick inside that save, and the await after the abort (`crates/chan-library/src/tenant.rs:266-277`) then waits, and with it the terminal tenant's shutdown and the drain, until the mount answers. The owner's contract for the quit is about a mounted root, so this is outside it unless the chan home shares the mount with a workspace. The review did not check whether another write under the chan home on the quit path would block first.

## Desired contract

No wait of the quit drain depends on the chan home's filesystem: a save the drain can meet runs off the runtime worker or within a bound.

## What to do

Move the registry's save off the runtime worker, onto the blocking pool or a writer thread of the registry's own, or bound it, and read the other writes under the chan home that the quit path can meet for the same shape. Pin it with the save held while the host drains. Red first: with the save held, the drain does not return.

## Boundaries

`crates/chan-library/src/terminal_sessions.rs` (the pruner), `crates/chan-library/src/host.rs` (the window reaper), `crates/chan-library/src/windows.rs` (the save), and their tests. The design documents say what the drain waits for if that changes.

## Acceptance

1. With the registry's save held inside the pruner's tick, the host's drain returns within its bound, pinned by state and not by time alone.
2. A reap in a healthy home still saves the registry, and the row is gone from it.

## What shipped

Built on 2026-10-05 on the v0.102.0 integration branch and not on `main`, in a range the lead accepted on its report, its status files (both reds at their own labels at committed shas with a clean tree, five mutations restored by hash, four series of 200 runs in parallel and on one CPU with no red, the closing gate green over twelve steps at the tip with the symlinked-temp suite and both Windows clippy lines) and an independent review of its whole diff, which found nothing above medium: two mediums, sent to the next range, and nine lows. This record was written that day from those.

The pruner's part of the contract is built. Each tick, the reap of exited sessions and the prune of idle ones, runs on the blocking pool, and the pruner awaits it beside the tenant's stop signal, so no second tick starts beside one still running (`spawn_pruner`, `crates/chan-library/src/terminal_sessions.rs`). A stop that arrives while a tick is inside the window registry's save closes every live session and ends the pruner. The task owner's shutdown awaits the pruner's own handle and nothing of the tick, so the terminal tenant's shutdown and the host's drain go on, where the await after the abort waited for the mount: the wait is removed and not moved. A held save keeps one blocking thread, and with it the terminal registry and the window registry, until the chan home answers; the tick then finishes that save, finds its other sessions gone and ends. No session is closed twice, since every closer removes a session from the map under one mutex before it closes it, and a dead session the tick had listed and the stop closed first is closed as a shutdown and keeps its window row, as when a stop comes before a tick. The window reaper and the save itself are unchanged, and `crates/chan-library/design.md` says that the pruner awaits its window save beside the stop signal.

Pinned red first (`pruner_shutdown_closes_live_sessions_while_reaper_is_held`): the window reaper's callback, from which the host saves, is held once a dead detached session has entered the first tick, and the task owner's shutdown is asked beside it. Before the fix the shutdown had not returned after seven seconds; with it the shutdown returns while the reaper is still held and with the live session closed, so the pin holds by state and not by time alone. The pin holds the reaper's callback in place of the save and asks the task owner in place of the host: that the tenant's teardown and the host's drain await nothing else of the pruner is read in the code and not run. Acceptance 2 is held by a guard the earlier code passes (`terminal_pruner_persists_a_reaped_window`, `crates/chan-library/src/host.rs`): in a healthy home the pruner's reap empties the registry, and `windows.json`, read back from disk, no longer lists the window. Three mutations red their assertions and are restored by hash: the tick put back on a runtime worker, the reap skipped, and the close left out of the stop arm beside a tick. Two series of 200 runs of the pin, in parallel and on one CPU, show no red. Not established without a run: that a process quits with a save still held. By reading, the CLI ends its runtime without waiting for a blocking task already started, and no run shows a quit ending over a chan home that does not answer.

What the row still asks. The contract is that no wait of the quit drain depends on the chan home's filesystem, and on a devserver under systemd two writes of the restart manifest, a file under the chan home, still come before the drain on a runtime worker: the final write at a graceful stop, and the stop of the debounced writer, which is awaited after its abort while it is inside a write. A chan home that does not answer still holds that quit, and its repair is the next range's. Read and not changed, outside the quit: creating a terminal reaps exited sessions on the caller's runtime worker, and the window actions of the library's routes save the window registry there, so a held home can hold those requests; and the desktop saves its overlay before its drain, read only as far as the call. From the review, for the next range: a tick's join error is raised as a panic whatever it is, a cancellation included, and the doc of the pruner and the design's sentence do not say what a stop leaves behind, the thread and the two registries a held tick keeps. The row stays at build for the restart manifest's writes at a devserver's quit.
