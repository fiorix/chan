## What was seen

A removal selects the registry row of the workspace that it is to remove, then takes the root's lock, then closes, purges, wipes and unregisters, and it does not carry the selected row across those steps. By the review's reading of the repair of [a-removal-unregisters-by-the-name-it-is-given](../done/a-removal-unregisters-by-the-name-it-is-given.md) (the range `2b5ebe6d1..1bd028bef`, built and not landed), a registered path that leads through a symlink, relinked twice, with a registration that runs between the selection and the lock, can make a forget act on another workspace than the one it selected: it closes that workspace, purges its windows, wipes the state that chan keeps for it, its index, graph, session blobs, app tokens and report, and unregisters it. The folder's own files are not touched. It needs two timed relinks and a concurrent registration. It was read and never reproduced, no test holds the interleaving, and the repair does not make it worse: the base selects by the name it is given and has the same window.

What the repair closes is the fault that needs no race, a forget of a workspace whose registered path was pointed at another registered workspace's folder; that is [a-removal-unregisters-by-the-name-it-is-given](../done/a-removal-unregisters-by-the-name-it-is-given.md)'s, accepted for v0.101.0.

**The design record.** A fix was designed from source, in four rounds of design and three independent reviews of source, and no line of it is written (`dev/v0101-team/followups/followup-Runtime-Lead-30.md` to `-33.md`, with the lead's rulings on them, `dev/v0101-team/followups/followup-Lead-Runtime-54.md` to `-56.md`, and `dev/v0101-team/reviews/review-Runtime-23.md` to `-25.md`, all in the development tree). It is a private claim, held in memory, that reserves the selected row from its selection to the end of the unregister, with a refusal and a retry when a claim conflicts: an internal outcome for a conflict, apart from the answer that a workspace is still releasing; a removal's progress as untouched or as committed to the close; a detach that verifies the selected runtime by the identity of its allocation; ownership of the restore attempts that are queued; and a conservative result for a stale cleanup, which closes nothing, unregisters nothing and answers retry. The last review's direction was accepted as a contract of source alone, by its own words no acceptance of behaviour, of a gate or of a release.

**Three findings that the design holds,** from the last review (`review-Runtime-25.md`, findings 1 to 3); what each means for a user is inferred from them:

- **The commit of a removal with no mount shares the guard of absence.** This is the forget of a workspace that is not mounted at that moment. A design that checks that nothing is served at the workspace's address, lets the lock go, and only then marks the removal committed leaves a gap in which another thread can publish a terminal or a workspace at that address, and the forget would then unregister, purge windows and write off state under what its user had just opened there. As designed the check and the commit are one locked step, and a forget that finds the address taken answers retry and changes nothing.
- **A selected runtime that has gone is left untouched.** This is the forget of a workspace that was mounted when it was selected and is gone or replaced when the forget comes to detach it. The forget answers retry with nothing changed, `workspace is still releasing; retry` with 503 and `Retry-After: 1`, and never falls through to the path of a workspace that is not mounted, where it would unregister or purge whatever is there by then.
- **The operation's share outlasts the request.** The claim on the row is owned by the teardown and the unregister that run in the background, and not by the request alone. If it ended with a request that its client left, its user could add the same folder again while the old wipe still deletes its index, graph, sessions, tokens and report, and the new registration would lose state or be unregistered. As designed a second add in that window answers retry until the old work ends.

**The costs that the design declares** (`dev/v0101-team/reviews/review-Runtime-24.md`, finding 5; `review-Runtime-25.md`, finding 5; `followup-Runtime-Lead-33.md`). A forget or an add can answer retry earlier and more often. A stale off or forget that cannot prove that it is alone leaves the runtime mounted and the row registered, and its user asks again. Four things are outside what the design guarantees, raised on 2026-09-30 as [four-gaps-lie-outside-a-removals-row-claim](four-gaps-lie-outside-a-removals-row-claim.md): an off of a workspace that is still starting can be acknowledged and the old attempt can publish a runtime afterwards; the overlay's snapshots are written as whole vectors, which can bring back a removed row or drop a new one; an idle devserver record can be taken for a new row at the same path; and the native desktop's snapshot writer.

**What was tried and set aside.** An in-memory receipt and a marker that could be revoked, meant to let a stale mount attempt close only the runtime that it had created, were rejected: the host publishes a runtime's routes before the host's call returns, so the marker cannot prove that nobody else uses the runtime (`review-Runtime-24.md`, finding 3). The conservative result above replaces them.

**What is stopped.** The order on this race first asked for a deterministic executable witness of the interleaving, red at the assertion that the other row is kept. Its work ended on a content-risk stop of the platform that the agents run on, before any report and with no change of the product, and the lead ruled that the stop is not worked around and narrowed the order to a design from source. Three proof drafts were prepared and never installed or run (`dev/v0101-team/evidence/Runtime/r34-geekom-DRAFTS.md` in the development tree). So the race is neither shown nor refuted, and by the owner's ruling of 2026-09-29 the executable work stays stopped.

## Desired contract

A removal acts on the registry row and the runtime that it selected, or it changes nothing and answers that its caller must retry. No relink and no registration between its selection and the end of its unregister makes it close, purge, wipe or unregister another workspace, and an add of the same folder while an earlier removal's wipe still runs answers retry and loses nothing.

Two costs of this contract are raised for the owner. Neither is repaired and neither is decided. The first: a workspace can stay mounted where nothing lists it and nothing reaches it. It takes the registry to stop holding a root while its open runs: by a removal that another chan process sharing the library makes, read in by the registry's reload before the devserver holds the workspace's lock, or by a registration that drops a superseded row. The superseded attempt then drops its record and leaves the tenant mounted with no record and no registry row. The devserver's list does not show it and the launcher's does not; a turn-off and a forget at its prefix answer 404; `chan close` of its path answers that it is not served; and it keeps the workspace's writer lock. It ends when the devserver stops, or when the path is added and turned on again, which hands the old tenant to the new registration. Before this build the attempt ran the host's removal there, the call this item is about, and nothing stands in its place. A forget asked of the devserver itself does not get there: the host's removal waits for the root's lock, which the open holds, and then closes what the open published. How wide the window is was not established.

The second: `chan serve` handed to a devserver whose mount of that folder is superseded while it runs is answered an error, with the sentence `chan-workspace: workspace is already open in this process; drop the existing handle first`, and the command falls back to serving alone, as it does on every error but another process's lock. Where the mount was superseded after its open, the devserver serves the workspace and holds its lock, so the fallback fails: the user reads that the local devserver could not mount this workspace and that a standalone server is starting, the first of which is untrue, and then that the workspace is held by another process and to run `chan serve --devserver`, which is what was just run. A second run succeeds, since a new attempt adopts the tenant. Where the attempt stood down before its open nothing is mounted, and the command serves on its own a workspace that was just turned off, or registers again and serves one that was just forgotten.
