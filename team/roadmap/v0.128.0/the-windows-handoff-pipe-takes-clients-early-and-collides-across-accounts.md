# The Windows handoff pipe takes clients before it is served and collides across like-named accounts

Status: raised on 2026-11-10 by the review of v0.127.0's single-instance guard before its GA, from reading; nothing here was run on Windows. Accepted for v0.128.0 by the owner the same day. Not started.

## What is there now

On Windows the desktop's single-instance claim is the first instance of the handoff named pipe (`claim_endpoint_at`, `crates/chan-server/src/handoff.rs`, `first_pipe_instance(true)`), taken at the start of a launch and served by the handoff listener only once the embedded server is up. Two readings of that code, neither yet run:

- A client can open a pipe instance as soon as it exists, so during the first desktop's boot every handoff client (`chan serve` through the shim, a bare launch's offer to open a terminal, the connect-only liveness probe) connects to a pipe nobody reads yet, where before v0.127.0 it met no pipe and answered "no desktop" at once. It then waits its whole reply budget (3 s for most requests, up to 75 s for a remote serve), and a request whose client gave up may be served when the listener starts, after the CLI already fell back to serving on its own.
- The pipe's name is `\\.\pipe\chan-desktop-<USERNAME>` with every character outside `[A-Za-z0-9_-]` removed, so `first.last` and `firstlast` share a name, as do a local and a domain account of the same name. The second account's claim is refused with access denied, which reads as held by a desktop (`ClaimError::Held`); its request to come forward cannot open the other account's pipe, so after 5 s the launch exits 1, and that account cannot start the desktop while the other runs. Before v0.127.0 that account lost only its handoff listener. A local user can hold the name on purpose with the same effect.
- The listener's accept loop ends when it cannot create the next pipe instance (`handoff re-arm`, `start_claimed_listener`), and the instance it then drops is the last one, which is the claim: the desktop keeps running with the endpoint free, and a second launch boots beside it. v0.127.0 keeps the claim when the listener fails to start; this is the same loss while it runs.

## Desired contract

1. A handoff client that reaches a desktop still booting gets an answer, or a prompt "not ready", instead of waiting out its budget, and no request is served after its client gave up.
2. One account's desktop never stops another account's desktop from starting: the endpoint's name is unique per account (the account's SID, not a filtered user name), or a holder whose pipe this account may not open is treated as no holder, with the desktop running without a handoff listener and saying so in its log.
3. One desktop per account still holds: two launches by one account end in one desktop, and a listener that stops serving does not free the endpoint while its desktop runs.

## Boundaries

- The Windows arms of the claim, the listener and the client in `crates/chan-server/src/handoff.rs`, and their callers in `desktop/src-tauri/src/single_instance.rs`. The unix arms are unchanged.
- A name change is a wire change between a desktop and a CLI of different releases on one machine; the item says what a v0.127.0 CLI meets beside a v0.128.0 desktop, and the reverse.

## Acceptance

1. First, a reading on the Windows VM that settles both claims: a handoff client started while the desktop boots (the time to its answer, and whether its request is served late), and a second account whose name collides after filtering.
2. Tests compiled and run on Windows for whichever contract the reading finds broken, red at the v0.127.0 code.
3. On the Windows VM: `chan serve` during a desktop's boot answers within a second or is served by the desktop, never both; two accounts each run their own desktop.

## Reading on the Windows VM, 2026-11-11

Read on the Windows 11 test VM at `470be4fe9` with the installed NSIS build (the kit, logs, scripts and screenshots are the round's evidence, `evidence/Desk128/winpipe-1/`). A client during the boot: the claim's pipe instance takes a client before the listener runs. A client that writes its request and gives up before the listener starts is served afterwards: with the boot stretched to 6 s (the desktop suspended at its claim), the desktop opened the window of a `chan serve --desktop` that had exited 2.5 s earlier, and when that client's own fallback held the workspace the desktop met its lock instead. A bare `chan serve` never waits its reply budget: its connect-only probe takes the claim's only instance, the handoff meets a busy pipe for its 1.5 s retry and serves standalone beside the booting desktop, which a natural 2.2 s boot already showed for one of two clients. A second launch during a boot longer than about 8 s reports "Chan is not responding" for a desktop that is only booting. Natural boots on the VM took 0.4 to 2.2 s from the claim to the listener. Accounts: `ch.an` cannot start Chan while `chan`'s desktop runs (both calls get access denied, the guard reads it as held, the launch shows "Chan is not responding" after 5 to 6 s and exits 1, and its CLI shims are never installed, since the desktop installs them during the boot the guard refused), and the reverse is the same. Because the pipe keeps the default DACL, which gives Administrators full access, an elevated process of an account whose name filters alike reaches the other account's desktop: an elevated `chan serve` opened its workspace in the other account's session, served as that account, and an elevated launch exited 0 "came forward" while the other account's desktop came forward. One desktop per account held in every reading, a suspended desktop keeps the endpoint, and a client that connected and left before the first accept did not stop the listener. Contracts 1 and 2 are broken on Windows; contract 3 holds where read; the re-arm failure arm (`start_claimed_listener`) was not reachable from outside without a hook. Not read: a local and a domain account of one name, a second session over RDP.

## Built: the claim and the listener, 2026-11-11
On Windows the desktop's claim is the first and only instance of a pipe of its own, `<endpoint>.claim`, which no client opens, held by the listener's handle for the process's life. The endpoint's pipe is created when the listener starts. A client during the boot meets no pipe and answers at once: `chan serve` prints "chan: chan-desktop is starting and cannot open the workspace yet; starting a standalone server." and serves on its own, and nothing it sent is served afterwards. A second launch reads a claim without the endpoint's pipe as a desktop still booting and waits for its listener up to thirty seconds before asking it to come forward, so a long boot no longer ends in "Chan is not responding". A listener whose re-arm fails logs once and stops while the endpoint stays claimed. Whether each pipe exists is read from the `\\.\pipe\` listing, which opens no instance, and an unreadable listing is no reading of a boot. A desktop of the previous release, which holds the endpoint pipe with no claim pipe, is asked to come forward rather than booted beside. Tests compiled and run on the Windows VM are red at the base for the late request, the probe during the boot, the failed re-arm, the long boot and the claim that never listens, and green at `b086679e3`. On the VM with the installed build: `chan serve` during a boot stretched to six seconds printed the starting line within 0.44 s and nothing was served after the resume, where the base opened the window of a client that had exited and met a fallback's lock eight times; a second launch during a ten-second boot came forward with exit 0, where the base showed the dialog at 8 s; a Start menu launch beside a desktop that stopped answering still shows the dialog after 8.5 s and exits 1. One desktop per account held in every run. Contract 2 (the endpoint's name, its owner-only security and the client's check of the server) is the next stage.

At the merge into the integration branch (`dev/v0128-team/evidence/Lead128/winpipe2-merge`): fmt; clippy on chan, chan-server and chan-desktop with warnings denied; the handoff tests (78), the single-instance tests (32) and the CLI desktop module (5); the marketing check; `make check-windows-test-target` in the gate guest; all green at d60468fec.

## Built: the account isolation, 2026-11-11

On Windows the handoff endpoint is named by the account's SID, `\\.\pipe\chan-desktop-<SID>`, with its claim pipe `<endpoint>.claim` beside it, the SID read once from the process token through a new Win32 boundary crate, chan-winpipe, so two accounts whose user names filter alike, or a local and a domain account of one name, have two endpoints. Both pipes, and every instance the listener arms, are created with a descriptor that grants their account alone (written as `O:<SID>D:P(A;;GA;;;<SID>)`, read back as `FA`; no entry for LocalSystem is needed), and every Windows client reads the account its pipe's server runs as (the server's process id, then that process's token) before it writes, so nothing is written to a pipe another account serves or whose server it cannot read. A claim pipe of the name that refuses this account access is another account's and is no holder: the launch boots without the handoff listener and says so once in its log. An endpoint pipe another process created first lets the claim be taken; the listener fails on it, and the desktop runs without its listener, keeping its claim. The claim stage's rule that a desktop of the previous release is asked to come forward rather than booted beside went with the SID name: the two names never meet. chan-server keeps `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`; the crate denies `unsafe_op_in_unsafe_fn` and each unsafe block is one Win32 call or the read of its result. The renamed endpoint is a wire change: a CLI of one release beside a desktop of the other, either way round, finds no desktop and serves on its own, and a launch boots a desktop of its own; the desktop rewrites the `chan` and `cs` shims at each boot and the installer stops a running Chan before it copies files, so the pair converges after an upgrade. Tests compiled and run on the Windows VM are red at the base for the SID name, the owner-only descriptors, the client's account check, an endpoint pipe without a claim pipe and another account's claim pipe, and green at the tip (chan-winpipe 6, handoff 48, single_instance 33; the Windows CI list, now fifteen tests, 15 of 15); nine of ten guard mutations red, the tenth inert by Windows' design (a pipe keeps its first instance's descriptor, and the listener always holds an instance). Not guarded by a test: a server whose account cannot be read is refused by the same arm (hard to stand in for on one account). On the VM with the installed build: `ch.an` started Chan from the Start menu beside `chan`'s desktop and got a desktop of its own with no message, and the reverse, where the first stage met "Chan is not responding" both ways; every create and open of the other account's pipes was refused, elevated or not; `chan`'s elevated `chan serve` and launch reached `chan`'s own desktop, where the first stage saw them reach `ch.an`'s. Left as they are, stated for this release: a process of another account that creates this account's claim name with a limit of one and grants this account access reads as a desktop holding the claim, so the launch waits the boot bound and exits 1 after about 35 s (it receives nothing); and a desktop that boots beside another account's claim runs without a claim, so a second launch boots a second desktop; contract 3 therefore holds when no other account squats the claim name. A follow-up that moves the Windows claim to an owner-only lock file under the account's profile, keeping the claim pipe as the listing's booting signal, would close both; it is the owner's call. The other pipe families (the devserver handoff's, the per-process control socket's, the MCP bridge's) keep their names and the default descriptor; chan-winpipe has what they need. The review of this stage is `dev/v0128-team/reviews/review-winpipe-t3.md`; the merge gate (6f75b3e2a) ran fmt, clippy on chan-winpipe, chan-server, chan-desktop and chan, the handoff and single-instance tests, shell-check, the marketing check, and the Windows target's lints in the gate guest; the Nix cargo vendor hash was harvested and pinned on the merged lock. The Windows VM candidate check at the milestone is the remaining reading on the merged tree.
