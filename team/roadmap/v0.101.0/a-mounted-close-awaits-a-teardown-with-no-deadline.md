# A close of a mounted root awaits a teardown that has no deadline

Status: raised for a decision on 2026-09-28 by the code map of what is left of the hung root and of the open with no bound (`dev/v0101-team/int30-docs/codemaps/services-hung-root-next.md` in the development tree, its fourth headline and its shape A4, read at `7957bccef`, nothing run), which found that no item has it as its scope. Read again in code at `b39274a1a` and not reproduced; that the recovery pass or the watcher's supervisor can be inside a call on a root that stopped answering when a close comes is inferred. Recommendation, the lead's: accept for v0.101.0, with the orders that are left of the hung root, in the map's shape A4, which gives the close the budget that a drain already has, at the cost of a close that reports completed while the writer lock may still be held.

## What was seen

A close of a mounted workspace takes its runtime out of the host, records a user's off in the workspace overlay, and then awaits the runtime's teardown before it answers (`close_workspace_impl`, `crates/chan-library/src/host.rs:3509-3577`: the runtime out at `:3543-3552`, the off at `:3553-3559`, the await at `:3567`). The teardown gives the tenant's tasks their grace, drops the tenant's keepalive, and then runs the workspace cell's clear and the writer lock's release check in one blocking hop (`shutdown_with_budget`, `:630-651`). A close passes no budget (`shutdown`, `:625-628`), so it awaits that hop with no timeout (`:652-662`). A drain of the host passes a budget and stops awaiting the hop at its deadline, five seconds from dispatch, leaving it to run in the background (`drain_tenants`, `:3683-3690`; `:652-659`; `WORKSPACE_SHUTDOWN_RELEASE_TIMEOUT`, `:49`). For a close the deadline bounds only the lock check inside the hop (`:641`, `:648-650`), and not the clear before it. The design document says so: an individual close awaits the cell's teardown even when it passes that deadline (`crates/chan-library/design.md:44`).

The clear joins two threads that can each be waiting on the root, as the hop's own comment says (`host.rs:645-646`). It stops and joins the workspace's open-time recovery worker (`CellHandle::clear`, `crates/chan-server/src/lib.rs:1568-1586`, at `:1577`; `Workspace::stop_open_recovery`, `crates/chan-workspace/src/workspace.rs:1246-1253`), and it then drops the watch handle, whose drop stops and joins the watcher's supervisor thread (`lib.rs:1578`; `WatchHandle::stop` and `Drop`, `crates/chan-workspace/src/watch.rs:1372-1392`, `:1420-1424`). The supervisor stats each watched root at its cadence, at most 250 ms apart (`watch.rs:681-695`; `WATCH_RETRY_INTERVAL`, `:314`). That either thread is inside a call on a root that has stopped answering when a close comes is inferred: nothing was run, and the stall seam holds the recovery worker at its start (`workspace.rs:945-948`) but has no point in the watcher's stat.

A close by root holds the root's lock for its whole length, the teardown included (`close_workspace_for_root_impl`, `host.rs:3295-3310`), so while such a teardown waits, every later open, close and removal of that root waits behind it (the open's lock, `:1459`; the removal's, `:3406`). A caller that gives up drops the close's future: the hop keeps running on the blocking pool, and the runtime's drop does not dispatch the clear again, since the hop was started (`:644-651`, `:669-673`); what a cancelled teardown leaves is in the design document (`design.md:44`). A caller that waits waits as long. [a-control-socket-close-outlives-its-client](a-control-socket-close-outlives-its-client.md) names this wait for the control socket, whose close is never given up, and [the-quit-drain-can-hang-on-a-recovery-pass](the-quit-drain-can-hang-on-a-recovery-pass.md) names in what it shipped that a close was left unbounded when the drain was bounded; neither has the close's own teardown as its scope.

The one test that holds a cell's clear, with a stand-in cell whose clear waits on a channel, drives the drain alone (`host_drain_leaves_a_stalled_clear_and_shuts_down_healthy_tenants`, `host.rs:7920`; `HeldClearCell`, `:7894-7917`, which no other test uses); no test holds an individual close's teardown.

## Desired contract

A close of a mounted root answers within a stated bound whatever the root's filesystem does, and a later open, close or removal of that root is not held behind a teardown that does not end. What the close answers when its teardown has not finished by then is decided with [a-close-answers-before-the-writer-lock-is-free](a-close-answers-before-the-writer-lock-is-free.md).

## What to do

The map's shape A4: the individual close passes the drain's budget to the teardown (`shutdown_with_budget(Some(..))`, as `drain_tenants` does, `host.rs:3683-3690`), so it stops awaiting the hop at the deadline and leaves it to run. Its cost, as the map reads it: the close reports completed while the writer lock may still be held, and the next open of the root answers that it is still releasing until the teardown returns (inferred). That is the first outcome [a-close-answers-before-the-writer-lock-is-free](a-close-answers-before-the-writer-lock-is-free.md) raises for a revalidation whose caller left, where a close already reports completed once its release check's deadline passes (`design.md:34`); this shape brings it to every teardown that does not end, so the two are decided together. Red first, as the map proposes: mount a workspace that owes an open-time recovery pass, hold that pass in the stall seam, and close the workspace by root; today the close does not answer (inferred). Or hold the clear with the stand-in cell at the host's level.

## Boundaries

`crates/chan-library/src/host.rs` (`close_workspace_impl` and `HostedWorkspaceRuntime::shutdown`) and its tests, and `crates/chan-library/design.md`. Not `CellHandle::clear` in `crates/chan-server/src/lib.rs`, `stop_open_recovery` or the watcher, which are the joins the bound waits on, as the quit drain's item found. The close's registry lookup and the removal's unregister, which take a thread per caller that gives up, are [a-hung-root-takes-a-thread-per-expired-caller](a-hung-root-takes-a-thread-per-expired-caller.md).

## Acceptance

1. A close of a mounted root whose open-time recovery pass or whose teardown is held answers within the stated bound, pinned red first.
2. A later open and a removal of that root answer while the held teardown runs.
3. What the close answers then, and the design document's sentence on it, follow the decision taken with [a-close-answers-before-the-writer-lock-is-free](a-close-answers-before-the-writer-lock-is-free.md).
