## Owner ruling

Accepted on 2026-09-29 for v0.101.0. The owner accepted in one answer every recommendation the lead had put to them that day, and with that answer closed v0.101.0's intake under one rule: a raised item enters v0.101.0 only when it loses a user's data or weakens security and its fix is small and local, a test-only or infrastructure item only when it makes the release gate or a release job unreliable, and an item whose fix changes a contract or reopens excluded scope, or whose fault is a wrong state with a rare trigger, goes to v0.102.0. This item enters: its trigger is rare and its result severe, a close that sends HUP, TERM and KILL to a process that the session did not start, and its fix is one order in Rust. ## What was seen

- **The close signals the pid that the manifest named.** A session imported across a restart keeps its metadata's `child_pid` (`Session::from_imported`, `crates/chan-library/src/terminal_sessions.rs:4524`, the pid at `:4619`). Its close sends the session's writer thread a kill (`Session::close`, `:5226-5236`), which ends an imported child through `terminate_imported_child` with that pid (`:4755-4758`): since this process is not the child's parent, it sends HUP and TERM by the bare pid, waits up to a second for the pid to go (`IMPORTED_CHILD_EXIT_GRACE`, `:83-86`), and sends KILL (`:6142-6173`). Nothing checks that the pid still names the process that the session started.
- **The import carries no identity but the pid.** An import holds the session's metadata, its PTY master, its ring file, its replay and whether its manifest was sealed (`FdStoreSessionImport`, `:1042-1056`), and the metadata holds the pid and no start time (`FdStoreSessionMeta`, `:992-1026`). The devserver's restore takes each manifest session apart and drops what its pattern leaves out, the recorded start time among it (`crates/chan-server/src/devserver/fdstore.rs:624-631`), and checks only that some process holds the PTY's slave (`pty_master_has_live_slave`, `:677-697`).
- **The devserver's own cleanup pins the same identity.** The manifest records the boot id (`RestartManifest`, `fdstore.rs:79-94`) and each child's start time (`ManifestSession`, `:96-108`), and the cleanup of a skipped session signals only when both match, through a pidfd opened before the start time is read, so that a pid reused between the check and the signal cannot be reached (`signal_child`, `:929-953`; `crates/chan-server/design.md:27`).

So, by the records' reading, a shell that exits while a background job it started keeps the PTY's slave open leaves the session restorable, and once the shell's pid is reused, in the restart's gap or later in the session's life, a close of that tab sends HUP, TERM and then KILL to whatever process holds the pid by then, when this process may signal it. The probe of the order on the tests that signal showed the close's half of it: an imported session handed the pid of a `sleep` the probe had started ended that `sleep` at its close (`dev/v0101-team/evidence/Services/s34-probe.log`).

**This paragraph is corrected,** by the builder's reading of the fresh terminal's close at the integration base on 2026-09-30, read and not run: a fresh terminal whose shell exits is classified ended and its store names are removed at once, by `unpark_fdstore`, while the session and its replay stay for as long as something is attached, since the registry reaps an exited session only at an attachment count of zero and the reader does not consult the child's exit. So a shell that exits while a job holds the PTY's slave does not leave the session restorable, and the sentence above was wrong in its words: a terminal whose shell has exited is not carried across a restart. The close's half is as the records read it, and the pid's reuse the same; the ruling the corrected reading leads to is under What shipped.

**One more thing a fix has to hold,** by the report's reading: the manifest's writer reads each child's start time from `/proc` at every write (`fdstore.rs:263-278`, the start time at `:273`), so a manifest written after the pid was reused records the new holder's start time, and a check against it would pass for that holder.

## Desired contract

The close of a restored terminal signals the process that its session started and no other: the import pins the child by the boot id and the start time that the manifest recorded, and the close signals through that pin.

## What to do

A suggestion from the report: carry the manifest's start time into the import and onto the session; at the import, open a pidfd for the pid and compare the process's start time and the boot id with the manifest's, as `signal_child` does; end the child through that pidfd at the close; and write the start time the session was imported with into later manifests rather than reading `/proc` again. What a session whose child does not match becomes, a session with no child to signal or one the restore skips, is for the plan. Red first: an imported session whose recorded start time does not match the process holding its pid, closed, leaves that process alive; today it ends it.

## Boundaries

`crates/chan-library/src/terminal_sessions.rs` (the import, the session's child, `terminate_imported_child`) and `crates/chan-server/src/devserver/fdstore.rs` (the restore and the manifest's writer), with their tests, `crates/chan-library/design.md` and `crates/chan-server/design.md`. The tests that hand an import a pid they did not start are [tests-signal-a-process-they-did-not-start](tests-signal-a-process-they-did-not-start.md)'s.

## Acceptance

1. A close of an imported session whose pid names another process by then signals nothing, pinned red first.
2. A close of an imported session whose child is the one the manifest recorded still ends it, pinned.
3. A manifest written after a restore keeps the start time that the session was imported with, pinned.
4. The design documents say how an imported child is identified before it is signalled.

## What shipped

The build is on the integration branch and not on `main`: six commits, eight files, about 1,229 lines, accepted on 2026-10-01 on an independent review that read every pin against the plan's table and the lead's rulings and found the fix what the item, the plan and the rulings ask for. The order's plan was ruled by the lead on 2026-09-30: an import whose recorded child cannot be verified is skipped through the existing skipped-session path, and an import with no child pid stays descriptor-only; the fix is built before any work on the restore; the identity code lives in chan-library and the server calls it; and reuse is modelled by a deterministic backend and by real pidfd closes of children the tests own, with no pid-namespace or systemd experiment. Lines at the integration branch's tip.

- **The import pins the child before anything else of the session exists.** A new Linux-only module holds the recorded identity, the manifest's boot id and the child's start time, and one check that opens a pidfd for the pid before it reads `/proc/<pid>/stat`, so that a pid reused between the read and the signal cannot be reached, and refuses, in order, a missing recorded boot id, a boot id other than the local one, a missing recorded start time, a pid of zero or over `i32::MAX`, a failed open, an unreadable stat and a differing start time, dropping the descriptor on a refusal (`RecordedChildIdentity::pin`, `crates/chan-library/src/terminal_sessions/child_identity.rs:19`). The import calls it before the ring, the master's duplicates and the threads exist, and every refusal skips the import with its reason and no signal, through the registry's existing skip path; a numeric probe no longer exists outside the tests.
- **The close signals through the retained descriptor and no other.** The imported controller's kill sends HUP and TERM through `pidfd_send_signal`, waits the grace for the exit, then KILL and half a second more, and observes the exit by polling that descriptor, EINTR retried against the deadline (`terminate_imported_child`, `crates/chan-library/src/terminal_sessions.rs:6185`); an expired descriptor signals nothing, and a later holder of the pid is untouched.
- **The start time is captured once and copied.** A fresh child's start time is read once after its spawn, before the controller exists, and a failed read is never retried; the import carries the recorded time onto the session, the export copies it back, and the manifest's writer writes the stored time and reads `/proc` no more, so a manifest written after a pid's reuse records no later holder's time.
- **The import and the devserver's cleanup share the one check.** The cleanup of a skipped session calls the same `pin` and keeps its HUP and TERM through the returned descriptor; the server's own boot and start-time readers are gone. By a ruling of the lead's on 2026-09-30 the cleanup keeps its policy and no typed disposition is carried: the contract is that no signal is sent without a pin verified for that signal, so an import refused for a mismatch is refused by the cleanup's check too and nothing is signalled, while an import refused because `/proc` could not be read or the pidfd could not be opened may meet a cleanup whose check succeeds a moment later, and that child, the recorded one, gets what every skipped session's child gets; `crates/chan-server/design.md` says so, and a pin shows the mismatch refused at both.
- **The words.** `crates/chan-library/design.md` says that the import pins the child before adoption, that the retained descriptor carries HUP, TERM and KILL and the polled exit, that a skip comes before the ring or the threads, what a no-pid import is, how the start time is captured and copied, and the skip's cost; `crates/chan-server/design.md` says that the writer copies without reading procfs and that orphan names and inconsistent metadata authorize no signal; the changelog's entry is under Fixed. The design words say, as the ruling has it, that a fresh shell's exit removes its store names while an attached session and its replay can remain, and that such a terminal is not carried across a restart; they do not say that a fresh terminal is destroyed at its shell's exit.

**The cost, written and not built:** a restored terminal whose shell has exited while a job holds its PTY is no longer restored, so it loses its replay and its PTY at the restart, and a standalone terminal window is removed; a restored terminal whose shell cannot be shown alive follows the rule a fresh one already followed. By the review's reading, on a kernel or a sandbox that refuses `pidfd_open` every restore with a pid is skipped while fresh terminals work, and a manifest written by v0.100.0 can carry a start time its writer read after the pid was reused, in the window between the shell's exit and the controller's unpark, so the first restore under this build would pin that holder once; from then on the writer copies.

**The acceptance at the tip.** All four points are met: a close of an imported session whose pid names another process signals nothing, red first, with the mismatch refused at the import and at the cleanup; a close of the recorded child still ends it, with a child that ignores HUP and TERM ended by KILL and a close after the exit needing no reap; a manifest written after a restore keeps the time the session was imported with, across a second import from the written manifest; and both design documents say how an imported child is identified before it is signalled.
