# Two views disagree on a window's first terminal

Record before the release: raised on 2026-10-04 from the browser smoke suite's diagnosis, where check 107 was the one failure no run could sort into a check's readiness or a product fault; accepted by the owner on 2026-10-04 as an investigation of its own, apart from the suite's repair. `accepted | build`: the build is the reading with a verdict, and a repair of the check only if the verdict is the check's. ## Owner ruling

On 2026-10-04 the owner ruled that the browser smoke suite is repaired as its diagnosis proposes, with check 107 on an item of its own: an investigation before any repair is named.

## What was seen

Browser smoke check 107 (`scripts/e2e/browser-smoke/checks/107-terminal-rename-inventory.mjs` at `772cde1e2`, "terminal rename reaches inventory") opens three pages: A and B on one window, `terminal-rename-shared-107`, and C on another (`:264-266`, `:290-292`). It waits until A and B render the same pane ids (`:294-303`), opens a terminal in A and one in C through the page's own command (`:305-310`), waits until `cs terminal list --json` lists a session in each window (`:312-322`), and then waits until A, B and C each show the tab of their terminal (`:333-337`). Only after that does it rename anything.

Twenty recorded runs between 2026-10-03T10:14Z and 2026-10-04T12:50Z, at six commits of the integration branch, alone and in the suite: six green in 5 to 10 s, fourteen red in 31 to 44 s. Thirteen reds are one failure, `tab "Terminal-1" did not settle; last=[]`, thrown by the check's poll (`:34-36`) from the tab wait (`:90-100`) inside the `Promise.all` at `:333-337`; every one of the thirteen stacks reads `Promise.all (index 1)`, which is the wait on B (`:335`): the second view of the shared window showed no tab strip for 30 s after A had rendered its terminal (`:307`) and the roster listed both windows. The other two reds come earlier, at `terminal registration in both windows did not settle` (`:312-322`): the roster by then holds one row, C's, named `Terminal-1-2`. That suffix is what a proposed name gets while another live session holds it (`crates/chan-library/src/terminal_sessions.rs:1940-1949` at `2c9b165c3`), so A's `Terminal-1` was live when C's terminal spawned and had left the roster within the 30 s poll. The one run that captured each page at that failure shows A's pane empty, B's pane on its loading spinner, and C at a shell prompt in `Terminal-1-2`. The check reds alone (one of two alone runs), behind the three checks that precede it in the suite, and in whole runs, and it passes in each of those positions too: its place in the suite is not the cause. The diagnosis placed the divergence on a renamed terminal; the record places it at the first terminal, before any rename.

What the code says about that path, at `2c9b165c3`. A page arms its first session save once it is bootstrapped (`web/packages/workspace-app/src/App.svelte:271-276`) and sends it 750 ms later (`web/packages/workspace-app/src/state/store.svelte.ts:3220`, `:3343-3349`); an empty window serializes to nothing, so that save is `DELETE /api/session?w=<window>` (`:3355-3373`, the DELETE at `:3369`). The server broadcasts `session_changed` with `deleted: true` (`crates/chan-server/src/routes/sessions.rs:91-97`) and, before answering, reaps every terminal session of that window (`:197-222`, `forget_window` at `:222`; `terminal_sessions.rs:2720-2745`). A peer that receives the `deleted` frame seeds its save dedupe with its current serialization and sends nothing for a change it had not yet sent (`store.svelte.ts:3407-3419`). The check opens its terminals within a few seconds of B's load, and its barrier at `:294-303` proves that two views render the same pane ids, not that either is past its first save. By reading, both failures fit the race recorded in [a-windows-first-save-can-swallow-a-peers-unsent-split](a-windows-first-save-can-swallow-a-peers-unsent-split.md) on a window that holds a terminal: B's routine first DELETE lands after A's terminal exists and reaps it, or lands around A's layout save so that B never learns of the tab. No run has yet logged when each page's first save and A's first layout save answered, so this is a reading and not a verdict.

## Desired contract

A reading that says, from runs and not from the code alone, whether check 107 lacks a readiness barrier or the product fails to carry a window's first terminal to its second view, and what each red in the record was. If the check lacks the barrier, the check gets it and passes alone and in the suite; if the product fails, the fault is named with its evidence and the check stays red until it is fixed.

## What to do

First, without changing any wait or assertion, make each failure of the check say which view it waited on and capture, at the failure, each page's tabs and pane ids, the roster of both windows, the window's saved session, and a log of every `/api/session` request each page made with the time its response arrived; take a shot of each page before the check closes them, since the runner's failure shot is of its own page. Then run the check alone five times and once behind the three checks that precede it in the suite, and read the logs: if in every red the second view's first DELETE answered after the first view opened its terminal, and in every green before it, the verdict is the check's readiness and the repair is the barrier check 123 already has, each view past its own first session save before any terminal is opened, with the pane-id wait kept; if a red shows both first saves answered before the terminal opened and the second view still without the tab, or the first view's session gone for another reason, the verdict is the product's, no barrier is added, and the reading names the behaviour for an item of its own.

## Boundaries

`scripts/e2e/browser-smoke/checks/107-terminal-rename-inventory.mjs` and the suite's README sentence that describes its barrier. No product line changes under this item; the first-save race itself belongs to the item linked above, and a product fault the reading finds beyond it gets an item of its own.

## Acceptance

1. The reading exists, cites every recorded run and every line at its commit, classes each red, and ends with one verdict: the check's readiness or the product's.
2. If the verdict is the check's: the check passes alone five times and behind the checks that precede it in the suite, and in the whole suite; a copy without the barrier still reds; and the README's sentence on its barrier says what the barrier proves.
3. If the verdict is the product's: no change to the check, and the reading names the product behaviour with the captures that show it.
