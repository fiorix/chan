# A ghostty find loses its match on a resize

Status: shipped in [v0.128.0](../../release/release-v0.128.0.md).

Record before the release: raised by the v0.127.0 find follow-ups and accepted by the owner for v0.128.0 on 2026-11-09, when he kept the follow-ups' fallback for v0.127.0; built on 2026-11-11 (see Built). It touches the ghostty widget, so it lands on whichever widget is in place when it is built, before or after the [ghostty-web-ng item](../v0.129.0/chan-drives-ghostty-web-from-outside-through-its-private-internals.md).

## What is there now

In v0.127.0 find in a ghostty terminal hears every resize (`GhosttyViewportController.observeChanges`, `web/packages/workspace-app/src/terminal/ghosttyViewport.ts`), and on one it drops the highlight it wrote before ghostty-web repaints the new grid: the count keeps its total and shows no index (`? of n`) until Enter, Shift+Enter or a find command selects again, Enter then lands on the newest match and Shift+Enter on the oldest, and the viewport is not moved. A selection the user made stays. This is the fallback the follow-ups' work order allowed; the owner kept it for v0.127.0 on 2026-11-09.

The fallback was taken because libghostty-vt 1.0 drops its search's selected match on every resize, even one that changes only the rows and reflows nothing: after the next feed `index` and `active` are null and the scan restarts, and when it completes the total and the match list equal a fresh search's (the follow-ups' probe, kept with the round's raw data). `ghosttySearch.test.ts` pins that behaviour, so a later core that keeps the selection across a resize turns it red. The 1.0 wasm exports tracked grid references (`ghostty_terminal_grid_ref_track` and the calls around it), which follow their cell through a reflow; chan's binding (`src/terminal/ghosttyVt.ts`) does not bind them.

## Desired contract

1. After a resize, with or without a reflow, the match that was active stays active and highlighted once the scan has found it again, the count shows its index, and a match that was on screen before the resize is on screen after.
2. While the rescan runs the count shows a partial total and no wrong index, and a step pressed meanwhile behaves as a step does during any scan.
3. If the tracked position no longer holds a match (its row was pruned, or a resize split the text so it no longer matches), find falls back to today's behaviour: no highlight, `? of n`, the next step from the newest or oldest.
4. A selection the user made is never taken over, as today.

## Boundaries

- The fix tracks the active match's position through the core's tracked grid references and re-selects it after the rescan; no change to the core.
- Highlighting every match stays deferred.

## Acceptance

1. Vitest over the vendored core: a narrower and then wider resize that reflows the active match's line, and a rows-only resize, each keep the same match active with its index once the scan completes; a pruned or split match falls back as today.
2. Check 211's resize stage expects the same `k of 100` on the same row's match after the page narrows and widens, instead of `? of 100`.
3. The bound tracked references are freed with the search and the terminal, with a test that counts them.

## Built, 2026-11-11
Find in a ghostty terminal keeps its active match through a resize. libghostty-vt 1.0 still forgets its search's selected match on every resize, one that reflows nothing too, and scans again. chan's binding now binds the core's tracked grid references (`VtTrackedCell` and `VtTerminal.trackCell` in `web/packages/workspace-app/src/terminal/ghosttyVt.ts`; their four exports join the binding's required ones), and the find controller (`terminal/ghosttyFind.ts`) keeps two of them on the active match's first and last characters, which the core moves with their text through output, pruning and reflow. On a resize the highlight moves to the cells' new place before ghostty-web repaints, unless the user has a selection of their own (contract 4), and a match that was on the screen when the resize began, read before the core takes the new size (`VtTerminal.beforeResize`) against the old row count (`GhosttyViewportController.shows`), is scrolled back onto it if the reflow took it off (contract 1). Once the rescan lists the match that starts at the kept first cell (`VtSearch.indexOfMatchAt`), the controller selects it again by walking the core's selection there: the core has no call that selects a given match and each step costs a feed of the terminal (0.2 to 2.2 ms measured), so the walk runs a frame's budget at a time from the nearer end of the matches found so far, and while the scan runs that end is where the scan has just passed. Until it lands the count shows the total so far and no index and the highlight stays; steps pressed meanwhile wait behind it and apply from it (contract 2). The match is lost, with the v0.127.0 behaviour (no highlight, `? of n`, the next step from the newest or the oldest match), when its first cell's row is pruned, when the finished scan has no match starting there (the alternate screen crops its rows on a resize, which can cut one), when the walk goes once round without meeting it, or when `FIND_WAIT_CAP_MS` passes after the resize; a walk that gave up resets the needle so that the core holds no match (contract 3). The cells are freed with the search and with the terminal (acceptance 3).
Verified by vitest over the vendored core, ghostty-web's SelectionManager and the viewport controller (acceptance 1): narrower and wider reflows of a match's line and a rows-only resize keep the same match highlighted and selected again with its index; over a 20,000-line history the rescan's frames show partial totals with no index; pruned, cut, capped and once-round matches fall back; a user's selection stays theirs; the walk starts from the nearer end and starts again on a second resize; the tracked cells are counted through a search's life and the terminal's free; a mounted TerminalTab keeps `2 of 3` through a resize and steps on to `3 of 3`. With the controller at the base those tests fail 15 times at their assertions, and 37 of 38 mutations of the binding's, the controller's and the viewport's guards fail named assertions; the one that survives (a HISTORY point written for a SCREEN one) is equivalent on this core, which reads both alike. Check 211's resize stage (acceptance 2) now expects the same `12 of 100` on row 4899 after the page narrows and the same `13 of 100` on 4799 after it widens, Enter going on from each to the next older match: red at the base, green ten runs alone and with checks 94, 204, 216 and 223 in an isolated guest on headless Chrome, where make web-check is green too. The web app's design document and the manual's terminal page say so. Nothing here needs the owner's Mac.
