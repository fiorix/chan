# The quit drain can hang on a mounted root's recovery pass

Status: shipped in [v0.101.0](../../release/release-v0.101.0.md).

Record before the release: accepted for v0.101.0 by the owner on 2026-09-26; raised during v0.101.0 on 2026-09-26 by an independent reading of the fix for [a-hung-root-stalls-desktop-close-and-quit](a-hung-root-stalls-desktop-close-and-quit.md) before it landed, read in code and not reproduced; a source reading against `main` at `ef33cb0f3` and that fix.

## Owner ruling

Accepted on 2026-09-26 as the lead recommended: a quit finishes within a stated bound whatever a mounted root's filesystem does; a recovery pass that does not stop within it is abandoned, not awaited; pinned with a stalled recovery pass.

## What was seen

The desktop's quit starts a drain that awaits every runtime's shutdown. A runtime's shutdown clears its workspace cell, which calls `Workspace::stop_open_recovery`, an unbounded `JoinHandle::join` run inline on a runtime worker. When a mounted root's open-time recovery pass (a full rebuild after a crash, for instance) is inside a call that hangs on that root, the drain never returns and the process never exits. It needs a recovery pass in flight on the hung root; the fix for [a-hung-root-stalls-desktop-close-and-quit](a-hung-root-stalls-desktop-close-and-quit.md) does not reach it.

Read in code on 2026-09-27 at `3f60c072d`, not reproduced: a second join with no bound follows the first by one line, and it needs no recovery pass. The drain spawns each runtime's shutdown (`drain_tenants`, `crates/chan-library/src/host.rs:3480-3505`), and `HostedWorkspaceRuntime::shutdown` clears the tenant's cell inline (`host.rs:634`). That clear (`CellHandle::clear`, `crates/chan-server/src/lib.rs:1566-1584`) joins the recovery thread (`workspace.stop_open_recovery()`, `:1575`) and on the next line drops the watch handle (`:1576`), whose `Drop` stops and joins the watcher's supervisor thread (`crates/chan-workspace/src/watch.rs:1420-1423`, through `stop` and `join_supervisor` at `:1373-1392`). That thread stats the workspace root with `symlink_metadata` on every idle tick, at most 250 ms apart (`watch_supervisor_loop`, `watch.rs:687-693`; `WATCH_RETRY_INTERVAL` at `:314`; the root from `Workspace::watch`, `crates/chan-workspace/src/workspace.rs:4242`), and reads its stop command only between ticks (`:626`, `:675`). A supervisor inside that stat on a root that has stopped answering keeps the join waiting, and every workspace tenant has a watcher unless its registration failed (`lib.rs:697-707`), so a quit can wait on any mounted root that stops answering, recovery pass or not. The `root_stall` seam cannot hold that stat: its only stall points are in `crates/chan-workspace/src/paths.rs` (`:428`) and `rooted_fs.rs` (`:233`), so no test that holds a root with it can hold the watcher there.

## Desired contract

A quit finishes within a stated bound whatever a mounted root's filesystem does; a recovery pass that does not stop within it is abandoned, not awaited.

## What to do

Bound the join in `stop_open_recovery` (or run it off the drain's path) and pin with a stalled recovery pass under the `root_stall` seam. Small; chan-workspace and chan-server. The second join read on 2026-09-27 moves the bound to the host's drain: see Boundaries.

## Boundaries

The bound belongs on the host's drain, in `crates/chan-library/src/host.rs` (`drain_tenants`, and `HostedWorkspaceRuntime::shutdown`, which runs the cell's clear inline on a runtime worker), not in `stop_open_recovery`: the clear holds both joins, and a bound on the recovery thread's alone leaves the watcher's. Tests there and in the desktop's hung-root tests, with a seam in `crates/chan-workspace` only if the stalled recovery pass the ruling asks for cannot be held without one. `CellHandle::clear` in `crates/chan-server/src/lib.rs` and `stop_open_recovery` in `crates/chan-workspace/src/workspace.rs` are the joins the bound waits on, not the place for it.

## What shipped

Landed on 2026-09-27, with the bound on the host's drain as the Boundaries say. The two joins are unchanged: `CellHandle::clear` still joins the recovery thread and then the watcher's (`crates/chan-server/src/lib.rs:1566-1584`), now on a blocking-pool thread the drain can stop waiting for. Lines are cited at `7504a5f9c`.

- **What a quit and a devserver stop wait for.** `drain_tenants` takes every runtime out of the host and starts all their shutdowns before it awaits any of them (`crates/chan-library/src/host.rs:3512-3541`). The desktop's quit reaches it through `serve::stop_all` and `EmbeddedServer::shutdown_all` (`desktop/src-tauri/src/serve.rs:129-144`, `desktop/src-tauri/src/embedded.rs:377-385`), and both of the devserver's shutdown sweeps call it (`shut_down_hosted`, `crates/chan-server/src/devserver.rs:2381-2382`). Each runtime's shutdown (`shutdown_with_budget`, `host.rs:624-659`) gives the tenant's tasks their five-second grace and then aborts and awaits the ones still running (`TenantTaskOwner::shutdown`, `crates/chan-library/src/tenant.rs:245-279`), drops the keepalive (`host.rs:631-634`), and runs the cell's clear and the writer-lock check in one blocking hop under one deadline, five seconds from dispatch (`WORKSPACE_SHUTDOWN_RELEASE_TIMEOUT`, `host.rs:47`; `:635-644`). The drain stops awaiting the hop at that deadline and leaves it running (`:645-651`).
- **The bound.** Beside a root that stopped answering, each runtime's shutdown returns within about ten seconds, at most five of task grace and five for the hop, with the runtimes shut down side by side; before this change the drain waited until the root answered. The desktop then gives each extension supervisor up to three seconds to stop, one after another (`embedded.rs:383`; `SUPERVISOR_SHUTDOWN_GRACE`, `crates/chan-server/src/extensions.rs:43`, used at `:270-278`). A devserver's first sweep runs beside the drain of the registrations it accepted before it stopped accepting, which keeps its own 30 seconds (`REGISTRATION_SHUTDOWN_DRAIN`, `devserver.rs:2352`, `:2374`). Two waits of the drain still have no bound, the joins of the aborted tasks and what drops on the runtime worker: [a-quit-can-hang-on-a-standalone-files-watch](a-quit-can-hang-on-a-standalone-files-watch.md).
- **A close is not bounded.** A close passes no budget and still awaits the hop (`shutdown`, `host.rs:619-622`, and `:653-655`); its lock check gets what remains of the same five seconds rather than five of its own (`wait_for_workspace_release`, `host.rs:4500-4515`).
- **A shutdown that never reaches the hop.** A runtime dropped before it dispatched its clear, because its shutdown was cancelled or never ran, cancels its tasks and hands the keepalive and the clear to a thread of its own rather than joining on the dropping thread; one that dispatched it does nothing more (`Drop for HostedWorkspaceRuntime`, `host.rs:662-683`).

What a quit that gives up leaves behind, and what it costs:

- The abandoned hop keeps joining the recovery pass or the watcher, and the workspace's handles and its writer lock stay held until the root answers or the process exits, as `crates/chan-library/design.md` (`:34`) and `desktop/design.md` (`:182`) now say.
- The teardown and the recovery pass write nothing to the user's root. The recovery pass reads the root and writes derived state under the chan home, and the next open schedules it again from the durable metadata, as both design documents state. The independent review of the fix read the recovery actions and found no call among them that writes to the root (`dev/v0101-team/reviews/review-Services-8.md` in the development tree); nothing was run to show it.
- A document or scene save that is already writing to a root that stopped answering when the quit gives up is not cancelled. The save runs on the blocking pool (`spawn_blocking` in the document flush, `crates/chan-server/src/doc_sessions/mod.rs:1852-1872`, lines at `b1ef073ae` in this bullet and the next), where the abort of the task that awaits it does not reach it, so it can still finish before the process exits. If the process exits first, before the save's atomic replace, the file keeps its previous contents and a temporary file can remain beside it: the save writes a temporary file beside the target, syncs it, and only then replaces the target (`write_text_if_unchanged`, `crates/chan-workspace/src/rooted_fs.rs:694-723`, into `atomic_write_stream_in`, `crates/chan-workspace/src/fs_ops.rs:1580-1593`). Whether the edit survives then depends on its recovery record under the root's `.chan/editor-sessions/v1/` (`recovery_path`, `crates/chan-server/src/doc_sessions/recovery.rs:185-192`). A reopened session loads a usable record before the disk (`doc_sessions/mod.rs:1527-1528`, `:1577-1579`), holds the edit as its text (`:661`), and is dirty when the disk still matches the record's baseline, clean when the disk matches the edit, and conflicted otherwise (`:579-593`; a scene compares parsed content the same way, `crates/chan-server/src/scene_sessions/mod.rs:419-431`). A record's existence alone is not enough: an unusable record is ignored with a warning (`recovery.rs:194-212`), a record over its budget is stored as a skipped marker (`:267-283`), and then the session seeds from the disk and the edit is lost; so is an edit whose record had not reached the root before the root stopped answering. Before this change the quit waited until the root answered and the save finished. This bullet is corrected: it said that the file keeps its previous contents and the edit is lost. The lane that built the Files watch bound read the code and found the edit lost only when the save does not finish and no usable record holds it, and the independent review of that range confirmed the reading (`dev/v0101-team/reports/report-Services-18.md`, "Recovery reading and design sentences", and `dev/v0101-team/reviews/review-Services-10.md`, its answer to question 6, in the development tree). Nothing was run to show it.
- Both design documents say so now (`crates/chan-library/design.md:34`, `desktop/design.md:184`). This bullet is corrected too: it said that neither did.

Pinned at two levels, each shown red before the fix in the lane's report (`dev/v0101-team/reports/report-Services-15.md` in the development tree):

- `host_drain_leaves_a_stalled_clear_and_shuts_down_healthy_tenants` (`host.rs:6916-6986`) holds one cell's clear on a channel beside a healthy cell, with one runtime worker and a 40 ms test budget, through both of the drain's arms, and asserts that the drain returns while the held clear has not finished and that the healthy cell was cleared once. It holds the whole clear with a stand-in cell, which the lane's report counts as the only cover of a hang inside the clear, the watcher's join included; no test holds the watcher's own stat of a root, since the seam has no stall point there (`paths.rs:428`, `rooted_fs.rs:233`, `workspace.rs:947`).
- `the_quit_drain_does_not_await_stalled_open_recovery` (`desktop/src-tauri/src/main.rs:8981-9050`) mounts a workspace that owes an open-time recovery pass through the desktop's embedded server, holds that pass in the `root_stall` seam, which now also holds the recovery worker before it claims a pass (`crates/chan-workspace/src/workspace.rs:946-947`, `crates/chan-workspace/src/paths.rs:435-438`), runs the production quit drain (`serve::stop_all`) under a 15-second watchdog, and asserts that the writer lock is still held when the drain returns.

What the pins do not show, from the independent review's findings 3 and 4, read in code:

- The host pin waits the test budget plus five seconds (`host.rs:6927`, `:6964`), longer than the production deadline, so a drain that ignored the budget it is given and used the five-second constant would still pass it on most runs. It shows a bound near five seconds, not that the drain honours its budget.
- In the desktop pin, the check that a root call is still held (`main.rs:9037-9040`) cannot fail once the stall was entered (`:9017-9020`), because the stall's list of entered calls only grows (`paths.rs:690`); the lock check (`:9033-9036`) is the one that shows the pass was not awaited. Its closing wait for the lock (`:9042-9049`) passes whether the hop or the `Drop` fallback cleared the cell, so neither pin shows on a real cell that the clear ran on the blocking hop.
