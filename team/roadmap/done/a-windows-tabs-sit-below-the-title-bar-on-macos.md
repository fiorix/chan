# A window's tabs sit below the title bar on macOS

Status: shipped in [v0.126.0](../../release/release-v0.126.0.md).

Record before the release: raised by the owner on 2026-11-08 with the code on `feat/titlebar-tabs` (`a9251bbdd`); accepted for v0.126.0; built by the owner and read on his Mac; merged into the integration branch through `feat/titlebar-tabs-windows` as `fd44595ed`, awaiting the release.

## What is there now

A chan-desktop window on macOS has AppKit's title bar as a row of its own above the page, holding the traffic lights and the title; every window is built transparent, and the desktop keeps the title bar opaque with a layer in the window background colour (`desktop/src-tauri/src/serve.rs:888-897`, `keep_title_bar_opaque`, applied to every window build at `1146`). Below it every pane draws its own tab strip at its top (`web/packages/workspace-app/src/components/Pane.svelte:1423`, with the empty stretch and the actions at `1692-1693`), so a window with stacked panes spends the title bar's row and one strip per pane on chrome. A strip whose tabs need more room than it has scrolls as a whole (`overflow-x: auto` on `.tabs`, `Pane.svelte:2292-2299`), so the A/B side toggle and the pane menu scroll out of view with the tabs. No view lists a window's panes with their sides and tabs: reaching a tab in another pane, or on a pane's hidden side, takes a press on that pane's strip or a flip of its side, and the workspace app binds no chord for such a list (`web/packages/workspace-app/src/state/shortcuts.ts` has no `Mod+Shift+A`). Lines cited at `fa08475b3`.

## Desired contract

1. Settings > Global > Title bar tabs, on by default, so a fresh install and a config that predates the field put the tabs in the title bar and only a stored `false` keeps the normal one, is chan-desktop's own preference (`titlebar_tabs` in the desktop's `config.json`), offered on macOS where the installed app advertises `set_titlebar_tabs`. It applies live to every window the desktop builds, whatever tenant the window shows, without a reload, and the newest change wins.
2. On, a window's title bar holds its page's tab bar. The title bar turns transparent over a full-size content view and hides its title; the traffic lights stay where AppKit puts them; the bar keeps their room at its left, with no room kept in fullscreen. The band is as tall as its 28 px tab row or the traffic lights' band, whichever is taller, so at 100 percent it is the native title bar's height, the row centres on the traffic lights while their band is the taller, and a zoomed-in page's row fills the band from its top with no empty strip under the tabs. The bar holds a button drawn as a small map of the window's panes, the active one lit, with a badge for the most urgent program status among the tabs the bar does not show, and the active pane's tab strip; the other panes draw no strip, so a focus change moves the strip and never resizes a pane. The bar's empty stretch drags the window and a double-click there zooms it; a press on a tab does not drag. The status pill sits below the bar, and the Pane strip auto-hide row leaves Settings while the bar is on.
3. The page decides and the desktop never turns the mode on by itself. A page load from another origin than the page that turned it on gives the window its normal title bar back before the new page lays out; a reload of the same origin keeps it, so the bar does not flash away and back. A window in the mode keeps its size across hide and restore. The Computers screen sits below the bar; while it is shown, the bar hides its button and strip and the title bar shows the window's title in AppKit's own place, following a rename.
4. The pane tree: `Mod+Shift+A` (Cmd+Shift+A on macOS, Ctrl+Shift+A elsewhere), also from a focused terminal, the bar's button and the command launcher's Show panes and tabs open a list of every pane of the window in reading order with its two sides and their tabs and program status marks. Typing filters the tabs by title, the keyboard walks the rows, and a pick makes the tab the one the window shows: its pane active, its side visible, the tab active and holding the keyboard. It works in every window on every platform, browsers included, and `chan serve --help` lists the chord.
5. A pane strip whose tabs overflow scrolls only its tabs. The A/B toggle and the pane menu stay at the strip's right end; a scroll button on each side pages the tabs and reads as disabled at its end; the wheel scrolls them sideways; a tab dragged over a button scrolls that way; the active tab stays in view. The same holds in a pane and in the bar.
6. In a browser a stored `chan.windowTabBar` of `1` or `0` forces the bar on or off in that profile; a forced bar outside the desktop's title bar is a plain top bar.

## Boundaries

- Linux and FreeBSD are unchanged: the title bar commands answer an inactive chrome there, the page never asks, Settings offers no row and windows keep their decorations.
- The four commands (`set_titlebar_tabs`, `titlebar_chrome`, `titlebar_tabs_preference`, `set_titlebar_tabs_preference`) act on the invoking window, except the preference, which is the desktop's own; they and Tauri's drag pair reach local and gateway-served windows alike, as the terminal background opacity pair does.
- Windows is the companion row, `a-windows-tabs-sit-below-the-title-bar-on-windows.md`.
- No change to the tab model, the strip's menus, drag and drop between panes, or Hybrid Nav.
