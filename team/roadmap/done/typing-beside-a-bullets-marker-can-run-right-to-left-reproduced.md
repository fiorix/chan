# Typing beside a bullet's marker can run right to left

Status: shipped in [v0.107.0](../../release/release-v0.107.0.md).

Record before the release: built on 2026-10-15, not shipped: the owner's sequence reproduced in a headless Chrome on the host and read green there after the repair, the tests red first; the readings, the repair and what it changes are under [Repair 2026-10-15](#repair-2026-10-15). Raised again by the owner on 2026-10-15, the day v0.106.0 shipped, with the steps that reproduce it; the record of the two bounded searches that did not find it is the closed item [typing-beside-a-bullets-marker-can-run-right-to-left](typing-beside-a-bullets-marker-can-run-right-to-left.md), withdrawn at [v0.106.0](../../release/release-v0.106.0.md). Owner the frontend seat.

Record before the release: raised by the owner on 2026-10-12 from the editor without exact steps. Two bounded searches that day, eighteen sequences and 216 keys on an item made by Enter and 48 cases and 886 keys on the typed-marker path, every key sent through Chrome's keyboard, found no reversal; closed at the v0.106.0 GA as not reproduced, the next step the owner's.

## What was seen

The owner's steps of 2026-10-15, in the owner's words:

> I figured out how to reproduce the bug with the bullet lists.
>
> When I open the editor, if I create a list with at least 2 bullet points, one on each line, I can trigger the bug by doing the following:
>
> 1. Placing the cursor on the beginning of the word in the second line
> 2. There has to be content on the line
> 3. Pressing arrow back twice, which moves the cursor to the beginning of the line
> 4. Pressing arrow forward once, to move past the bullet character, then pressing space
> 5. This will place the cursor again at the beginning of the word, but whatever you type will be from right to left, e.g. hello -> olleh
>
> We have the exact same bug on hyphenated lists, and similar bug on numbered lists except that on numbered lists the number gets separated from the dot and the characters are placed in between, which makes it a bit worse

As a key sequence, from two bullet items with text on both lines (`* one`, `* two`) and the caret at the start of `two`: ArrowLeft, ArrowLeft, ArrowRight, Space, then the letters of a word. The owner reads the caret at the start of the line after the two ArrowLefts, past the marker after the ArrowRight, and at the start of the word again after the Space; the letters then come out in reverse order, `hello` as `olleh`. The same on `-` items. On an ordered item the number and the dot come apart and the letters land between them.

What this sequence has that neither search sent: text already on the item, and a Space typed after the ArrowRight. The nearest sequences tried were `across-gap` of the first search (a new item with `abc` typed, ArrowLeft four times, ArrowRight, `hello`) and `owner-arrows` of the second (a fourth item typed and emptied by Backspace, ArrowLeft twice, ArrowRight, `hello`): no Space after the ArrowRight in either, and the second's item held no text (`evidence/Frontend106/bulrtl-plan.py` and `bulrtl-second-plan.py` in the v0.106.0 round's archive).

Read at source and run at the editor's state level on 2026-10-15, by a throwaway jsdom probe of `chanMarkdown()` with `chanDecorations()`, the arrows as `cursorCharLeft` and `cursorCharRight`, the Space and the letters as direct insertions at the selection, not kept. The editor replaces an item's marker with a widget and hides the whitespace between the marker and the text with a zero-length replace decoration (`bulletMarkerDecoration` and `decorateListHang` in `web/packages/workspace-app/src/editor/decorations/blocks.ts`), and neither range is atomic: `EditorView.atomicRanges` is registered for the wikilink, image, date, table, diagram and page-break widgets (`editor/widgets/*.ts`, `editor/commands/page_break.ts`) and not for list markers, as the editor design records for the task checkbox too (`editor/design.md`, "Atomic widgets"). So an arrow stops at every document position across the replaced ranges, and the owner's count of two is the bullet line's. On `* two` with the caret before `t` (offset 2 of the line), ArrowLeft lands at 1, between the marker and its gap, and again at 0, the line start; ArrowRight returns to 1; a Space typed there makes the line `*  two` with the caret at 2, strictly inside the hidden gap, which now covers both spaces. That position has no rendered place of its own: the view answers it with a position in the line element between its child nodes, not an offset in a text node (`view.domAtPos`), which is why the caret is drawn at the start of the word. On `2. two` the marker is two characters wide, so three ArrowLefts reach the line start and the ArrowRight after them lands between `2` and `.`; a Space there makes `2 . two`, the line stops parsing as a list item, the widget goes, and the letters land between the number and the dot (`2 hel. two` in the probe): the owner's ordered-list reading, reproduced in full at the state level. The reversal is not: with the caret inside the hidden gap, letters inserted at the selection land between the two spaces in the order typed (`* h two`, `* he two`, `* hel two`), the caret after each. What keeps the order there and loses it under the owner's hands is the browser's path for typed input, Chrome inserting the character at a caret placed in the line element beside a non-editable span and CodeMirror reading the change and the new selection back from the DOM; that path was not run for this record.

## Desired contract

Typed text goes where the caret is drawn: a caret shown at the start of an item's text puts the letters at the start of the item's text, in the order typed, with the caret after the last one. No arrow or space takes a list marker apart; an ordered item's number and dot stay together.

## Repair 2026-10-15

### The owner's sequence in Chrome, before

Run on the host in a headless Chrome 150 (`/usr/bin/google-chrome-stable`) driven through `puppeteer-core`, against the host's debug `chan` binary serving a throwaway workspace, with the web bundle built from the tree before the repair; the keys go through the browser's keyboard and the document text, the caret offset, the caret's DOM home (`view.domAtPos`) and the DOM selection are read after every key. The driver and both readings are kept with the seat's working files, not in the tree. The second line of each document and the caret offset in the document, before the repair:

- `* one` / `* two`, caret at 8 (before `t`): ArrowLeft 7, ArrowLeft 6, ArrowRight 7, Space `*  two` caret 8, then `h` `*  htwo` 8, `e` `*  ehtwo` 8, `l` `*  lehtwo` 8, `l` `*  llehtwo` 8, `o` `*  ollehtwo` 8. The reversal, as the owner reads it: every letter lands at 9 and the caret stays at 8.
- `- one` / `- two`: the same, `-  ollehtwo`. A nested `  * two` and a task `- [ ] two` under the same keys: `  *  ollehtwo` and `- [ ]  ollehtwo`.
- `1. one` / `2. two` with the owner's three ArrowLefts, caret at 10: ArrowLeft 9, 8, 7, ArrowRight 8 (between `2` and `.`), Space `2.  two` caret 8 (the space lands after the dot, at 9), `h` `2.h  two` caret 8 (the line stops parsing as an item, the marker widget goes and the caret sits in the raw text after `2`), then `2e.h  two`, `2el.h  two`, `2ell.h  two`, `2ello.h  two`: the number and the dot apart with the letters between them, the owner's reading. With two ArrowLefts the ordered item reverses like a bullet: `2.  ollehtwo`.
- Control: Space and `hello` typed at the text start with no arrows: `*  hellotwo`, in order.

The mechanism, from the readings and the view's source. After the Space the caret is at 8, strictly inside the hidden gap, which the empty replace now covers as one widget over `  ` [7,9); `view.domAtPos(8)` answers the line element at child index 5, between that widget's empty span and the buffer image CodeMirror keeps after it, a position the view marks imprecise (`DOMPos.after` with `precise` false, since 8 is not the widget's end). Chrome inserts each letter in front of the item text, document position 9, and the view's change reader, which takes no selection from the DOM when the head's DOM position is imprecise (`selectionPoints` is empty), maps the old caret through the change instead: 8 stays 8 since the insertion is after it. The next letter is inserted at 9 again, in front of the previous one. The state-level probe did not show this because it set the caret with the change in one transaction.

### The repair

Two parts, both in `web/packages/workspace-app/src/editor/decorations/` and installed together by `chanDecorations()`.

The walker (`walker.ts`) carries atomic ranges beside its decorations: a handler calls `ctx.pushAtomic(from, to)`, and the plugin provides them through `EditorView.atomicRanges` from its own `provide`, so an editor that installs the walker gets them with the decorations. `blocks.ts` registers one span per item line from the line start to the text start, the indent, the marker and the gap together (`decorateListHang`), and for a task item the hidden bullet prefix before the box and the gap after it; the checkbox itself stays a plain replace widget, as the design says, so a click still toggles the source and the caret can still stop beside it. One span and not three because atomic ranges move only a position strictly inside one: three adjacent spans would still let the caret stop at their joins, which is exactly where the owner's second ArrowLeft stopped.

A caret filter (`list_prefix.ts`, `listPrefixCaret()`), because atomic ranges cover arrow motion, deletion and pointer placement and nothing else. Home on an indented item stops after the indent (CodeMirror's Home goes to the first non-blank), a Space typed at the line start leaves the caret after it, inside the indent it made, and a restored selection can name any offset; each is a caret strictly inside the hidden prefix with the DOM home above. The filter is an `EditorState.transactionFilter` that moves such a selection to the prefix's edge: to the text start when the caret came from the line start or before (the typed Space, a restore from the document start), to the line start when it came from the text (Home), and a non-empty range grows to cover the prefix whole. Its geometry is the list commands' prefix regex (`parseListPrefix`), confirmed against the syntax tree (a `ListMark` at the line's marker), so a list-shaped line that is not an item, code in a fence or `2. two` under a paragraph, keeps every position, as the decorations leave it; a test pins that the filter's spans equal the walker's atomic ranges on every item shape.

### What changes

- Arrows: ArrowLeft from the text start lands at the line start in one press and ArrowRight from the line start at the text start; the one position inside `* ` and the two inside `1. ` are gone. On a task item ArrowLeft from the text start stops at the end of the box and then inside it, as before.
- A click on a marker glyph: `ignoreEvent` stays false, and CodeMirror's pointer placement now moves a hit inside the prefix to its nearer end (`skipAtomsForSelection`), so the click lands at the line start or the text start, never between the marker and its gap.
- Backspace at the text start removes the whole prefix in one press (indent, marker and gap; `  * two` becomes `two`), as `deleteCharBackward` does across any atomic range; before, it took one hidden character per press, the first press leaving a raw `*two` on screen. Delete at the line start does the same. `commands/list.ts` has no Backspace handling of its own, so nothing else moves. The pin in `blocks.test.ts` that backed over a hidden non-breaking space one character at a time (`- Text`, then `-Text`) is rewritten to the new contract: one press gives `Text`, the non-breaking space included.
- Shift+ArrowLeft from the text start selects the prefix whole, and so does Shift+Home. Home on an indented item alternates between the line start and the text start instead of stopping after the indent.
- Enter, Tab and Shift+Tab are unchanged: `continueListOnEnter` places the caret after the new item's prefix, which is the new span's edge, and the indent commands change only the document, so the caret maps to the moved text start. Their tests are untouched and green.
- The owner's sequence after the repair: the two ArrowLefts leave the item (the line start, then the end of the item above), ArrowRight returns to the line start, the Space goes in front of the marker as an indent space (the source line becomes ` * two`, rendered as before), the filter moves the caret to the text start, and the word lands there in order: ` * hellotwo`. With the three ArrowLefts of the ordered case the caret is on the item above and the word lands at its end, `1. one hello`, the second item untouched.

### Open

- Backspace at the text start of a nested item removes its indent with the marker, leaving a paragraph at column 0 that markdown reads as a continuation of the item above; Google Docs outdents first. A separate row if the owner wants that.
- The caret can still rest at the line start, in front of the glyph, where typed text goes in front of the marker as it does in the source: a Space indents the item, a letter turns it into a paragraph. Making the line start unreachable, with the text start as the item's first position and ArrowLeft from it leaving the line, is a model change for the owner to call.
- The repair is shown in Chrome on Linux. The owner's browser is not named, and WebKit (chan-desktop on macOS) reads a DOM change by a path of its own; the atomic range and the filter keep the caret out of the hidden spans in any engine, so the trap closes the same way, but no WebKit reading exists.
