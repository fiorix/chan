# The Rust review's 1,017 low findings were never triaged

Status: accepted for v0.100.0 by the owner as fold-along work. v0.99.0 disposed of every high and medium finding of the Rust review (22 and 177) and parked the lows untouched; the owner's triage ruling then was to keep them parked, and the ruling for this release is to fold them in as the round goes. The counts below are measured against `main` at `d3de0180b`; whether each finding is still a defect is not.

## What was seen

The review read the tree at `a7c3ce9d5` and its appendix holds 1,017 low-severity findings under 19 crate headings. Nobody has read them as a set. The review's own definition of low is cleanliness, but 198 of them are filed under kinds that describe defects rather than style, and nothing establishes that none of those is real.

The Rust tree moved by 498 commits across 277 files between the review and v0.99.0, so the list was re-located mechanically: `git diff -M -U0 a7c3ce9d5..d3de0180b` gives the changed hunks of every cited file, and a finding's region is its cited line plus or minus eight lines.

The left column is the defect-shaped 198. By crate, the largest bodies are chan-server (254, 53 defect-shaped), chan-workspace (174, 30), chan-desktop (118, 14), chan-library (76, 20) and chan (63, 16); the gateway workspace holds 143.

The working ledger, one line per finding with its kind, title, function, old and current location and status, is `dev/rust-review-lows.md` in the round's working tree, regenerated by `dev/v0100-team/prep/lows.py`. The defect-shaped findings are listed by crate with the live ones first; the rest are grouped by the file they sit in at HEAD.

## Desired contract

The lows stop being an unread list. Two rules, and neither is a sweep for its own sake.

**The defect-shaped kinds get a read.** Security, bug, concurrency, panic-risk and resource-leak (116 findings) are read against the current code in that order, crate by crate, and each ends with a disposition: fixed, refuted with the reason, or carried with the reason. Side-effect and error-handling (82) follow if the round has room. A fix that changes behaviour carries a test; a finding that turns out to be a medium in disguise is raised to the lead as its own item instead of being fixed quietly.

**The rest is folded by whoever is already in the file.** A lane that edits a Rust file for any other item takes the comment, stale, docs, idiom and small duplication findings sitting in that file in the same branch, as commits of their own, and marks them in the ledger. Nobody opens a file only to sweep it, and the structure and large duplication findings are not folded this way at all: they are refactors and need their own item.

## Boundaries

Every Rust crate in the root and gateway workspaces and `desktop/src-tauri`, bounded by the two rules above. No behaviour change rides in a commit described as a comment or idiom fix. The review's proposed fix is a suggestion, not the specification: v0.99.0 found several of the review's medium fixes wrong as written, and the same care applies here.

## Acceptance

1. Every security, bug, concurrency, panic-risk and resource-leak low has a disposition in the ledger at GA, each with the `path:line` it was re-verified at.
2. Every fix that changes behaviour lands with a test that fails without it.
3. Every Rust file a v0.100.0 lane edited for another item has its sweep lows marked folded or deliberately left, with the reason.
4. The release report records the ledger's counts at GA, and the findings still open carry to the next version as this item rather than being closed by the release.
