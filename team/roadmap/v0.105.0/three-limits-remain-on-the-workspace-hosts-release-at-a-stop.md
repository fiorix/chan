# Three limits remain on the workspace host's release at a stop

Status: raised for v0.105.0 by the owner's word of 2026-10-09, from the v0.104.0 release report's follow-ups; written down, not designed, not accepted for build.

## What was seen

The v0.104.0 report (`team/release/release-v0.104.0.md`, Follow-ups) asks to examine "the remaining host-release limits: a standalone stop can join its lane inline, a panicked or cancelled serve future can still drop a devserver host inline, and a queued blocking release may outlive the two-second wait". Each is a limit stated from a reading of the source; none is an observed failure. Line numbers below are at the released tree, `af2af8ac0`.

What a release runs: when the last handle on a workspace host goes, the drop of its transfer lane sets the shutdown flag, sets the cancel flag of each active job, clears the queue and joins its two workers with no bound (`BulkTransferLane::drop`, `crates/chan-server/src/bulk_transfer.rs` 528 to 547). The design read that a worker waiting for work returns at once and one inside a job returns when the job's work returns (`dev/v0104-team/reports/design-Runtime104-item3.md`, "Who holds a devserver's host"). v0.104.0 took a devserver's release off the thread that polls its stop: `releasing_host` (`crates/chan-server/src/devserver.rs` 3546 to 3573) awaits the serve body over a clone of the handle, then drops its own handle on a blocking task and waits `DEVSERVER_HOST_RELEASE_BOUND`, two seconds (3578), for it; when the wait ends first it logs that the release "did not finish within its bound; left in background" and returns the body's result (`team/roadmap/done/three-holds-keep-the-workspace-host-alive.md`, "Hold 2 landing 2026-10-08").

The two devserver limits are that function's own. Its doc says "A dropped future or a panic still drops inline; a queued blocking release may not start within the bound", and both design documents repeat it (`crates/chan-server/design.md`, "Devserver host lifetime"; the root `design.md`, the paragraph that opens "The process-long registry reload watcher"). Their reasons are in the design's section "Added 2026-10-08T07:03Z" and in its two reviews. A caller that drops the future of `run_devserver` before it completes drops the handle inline, which the CLI does not do, since it polls the run inside `rt.block_on` (`crates/chan/src/main.rs` 68). A panic in the body unwinds through the outer handle and drops it inline too, "so a panicking devserver could wait on a stuck lane worker while it dies" (`dev/v0104-team/reviews/review-Review104-item3-design-2.md`, "Notes for the code and its hand-back"). And the blocking task shares a pool capped at 32 threads (`MAX_BLOCKING_THREADS`, `bulk_transfer.rs` 41) with the work that is stuck in the trigger case, so with a full pool the release does not start inside its bound, the stop ends as it did before the change, and `rt.shutdown_background()` lets the process exit past it (`dev/v0104-team/reviews/review-Review104-item3-design-1.md`, "Hold by hold, what I read and found right").

The case in which an inline join costs anything is the design's own finding ("Hold 2: the registry reload watcher", the paragraph "The risk, stated as a finding of this reading"): a lane worker inside a transfer whose work does not return promptly once its cancel flag is set, as a read or a write stuck on a root that stopped answering, or a wait inside the job that does not watch the flag (a transfer's stall timeout defaults to 300 seconds). The join then does not return, the run does not return, the daemon's lock is not released, and under systemd the stop runs to its timeout and the kill. The design says its author did not read every wait in the transfer routes.

The standalone limit is older than v0.104.0 and was carried, not read. The v0.103.0 report (`team/release/release-v0.103.0.md`, Follow-ups) says a devserver's stop would join "its lane's two workers inline as a standalone serve's stop does". The v0.104 design lists "That a standalone serve's stop joins its lane inline" under "Not established", taken "from the v0103 record and one comment, not re-read", and its first review says it did not read the standalone serve's stop either ("Not established by this review"). The lead held it as an observation (`dev/v0104-team/reports/held-observations-Lead104.md`, row 4 of the index: "A standalone serve's stop may join its transfer lane inline") and disposed it as a known gap (`dev/v0104-team/reports/held-observation-dispositions-Lead104.md`, row 4). A reading of the released tree made for this item, and not run: `serve` (`crates/chan-server/src/lib.rs` 1613 to 1714) builds a lane of its own (1646), keeps the tenant's artifacts until it returns and has no bounded release; the artifacts' `bulk_transfer` field is documented as the lane's lifecycle owner, "dropping the last one shuts the lane down and joins them" (239 to 244); and `serve` has one caller, `crates/chan/src/serve.rs` 814.

What ran, on Linux: the release's pins with six mutants, among them an inline release on the error path seen on the poll thread and an unbounded release red at a held worker (`dev/v0104-team/reports/handback-Runtime104-hold2.md`, "Red pins, repair, and mutation proof"); and the real user-unit check `scripts/e2e/devserver-fdstore.sh`, whose six graceful stops took 0.007 to 0.043 seconds with all three holds in, the release holding the last host handle in each of the six at the second hold's tip (the closed item, "Hold 2 landing 2026-10-08" and "Hold 1 landing 2026-10-08").

Not established: that any of the three has happened, in a test or in use. None of the records named here shows a stop that hung or a panic during a stop, and the one release seen to end at its bound is the pin that holds a worker on purpose. Whether any transfer job can keep a lane worker past its cancel flag, which is what turns each limit into a hang or an unreleased host, has not been read. For the standalone serve, whether the artifacts' field is the lane's last handle when `serve` returns, and on which thread it drops, was not traced. The runs named here are Linux runs: the closed item records no macOS or Windows execution of the holds at their landing, and what the version's CI ran on those platforms was not read for this item.

## Desired contract

No stop waits without a bound on the join of a transfer lane's workers: not a standalone serve's, and not a devserver's whose serve future panicked or was dropped; and a devserver's bounded release starts whatever the blocking pool holds. The record establishes none of the three as reachable, so this item asks for a decision per limit after the reading below: bound it, or keep it as a stated cost with its reason, as the design documents keep two of them now.

## What to do

Read first, at source, and report to the lead before any design. For the standalone serve: follow `serve` to its return and say which handle of the lane is the last, on which thread it drops and what the stop of `chan serve` waits on after it, since neither the v0.104 design nor its review read that path. For the trigger: list every wait a transfer job can be in after its cancel flag is set, starting from the five production submit sites the review of the launcher hold counted, in `routes/files.rs`, `routes/standalone_fs.rs` and `routes/transfer.rs` (`dev/v0104-team/reviews/review-Review104-item3-hold1-range-1.md`, "The bar, point by point"), and say which return promptly. Then construct each limit with the fixture the release's pins already use, a lane worker held inside a job by the test: a standalone serve stopped with the worker held, a serve body that panics, a run whose future is dropped, and a release queued behind a full blocking pool. Each case either shows the stop blocked or the host left unreleased, or shows why the limit cannot be reached. A repair is designed only for a limit the lead rules to bound.

## Boundaries

`crates/chan-server/src/lib.rs` (`serve` and what it holds to its return), `crates/chan-server/src/devserver.rs` (`run_devserver`, `releasing_host` and its bound), `crates/chan-server/src/bulk_transfer.rs` (the lane's drop), the routes that submit to the lane where a wait is read, their tests, and the host-lifetime sentences of `crates/chan-server/design.md` and the root `design.md`. Not changed: the three holds as landed (the watcher that owns no host, the weak parker hook, the weakly held installed fallback); the bounded release on an ordinary exit and the sum its bound belongs to (the thirty-second registration drain, then two seconds, inside the unit's ninety-second stop timeout); the rule in the release's doc that no transfer job or result owns a host handle. Not this item's: the start-limit diagnostic of the fdstore fixture, which the same sentence of the report names, and what a client is told at a real stop.

## Acceptance

1. A written reading of the standalone serve's stop: the lane's last handle, the thread it drops on and whether the join it runs has a bound, each with its place in the source.
2. Every wait a transfer job can be in after its cancel flag is set is listed with whether it returns promptly; a claim that none can hold a worker shows its search beside a positive control.
3. Each of the three limits has a constructed case that fails on its own assertion when the limit is reached (the stop blocked past a stated bound, or the host still alive when the stop returned), or a stated reason it cannot be constructed.
4. The lead's ruling per limit is recorded here: bounded, or kept as a cost with its reason.
5. For each limit bounded: its pin red first on the behaviour and green after; fmt, clippy and the whole `chan-server` suite green at the commit in the owning guest; and `scripts/e2e/devserver-fdstore.sh` passing its nine cases with the graceful stop durations recorded.
6. The two design documents and the release's doc state the limits that remain after the change and none that was closed.
