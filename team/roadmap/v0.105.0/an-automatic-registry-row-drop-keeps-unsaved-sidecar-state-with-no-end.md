# An automatic registry row drop keeps unsaved sidecar state with no end

Status: raised for v0.105.0 by the owner's word of 2026-10-09, from the v0.104.0 release report's follow-ups; written down, not designed, not accepted for build.

## What was seen

The v0.104.0 report (`team/release/release-v0.104.0.md`, Follow-ups) asks to "Decide whether automatic workspace-row removal should preserve unsaved sidecar drafts indefinitely". The closed drafts item says what holds now (`team/roadmap/done/drafts-and-attachments-live-inside-the-workspace.md`, "Landing and validation 2026-10-08"): a State reset keeps drafts and drafts-trash, an Everything reset and a forget remove them, and "Automatic registry row removal retains unsaved sidecar state". That state is three directories of a workspace's sidecar in the chan home: `editor-sessions` (the editor's recovery records), `Drafts` and `drafts-trash`. The report's sentence names the drafts alone; the rule and the code cover all three.

The automatic path the record names is the drop of an appended row; whether another path drops a row by itself was not searched for this item (`crates/chan-workspace/design.md`, "Library", the bullet that opens "One directory has one row, with one exception that mends itself"; `Library::register_workspace_with_name` and `wipe_appended_row`, `crates/chan-workspace/src/library.rs` 707 to 725 and 886 to 983; line numbers here are at the released tree, `af2af8ac0`). A registration that does not hear, within the alias probe's two seconds, from a row whose stored root resolves elsewhere appends a second row for the directory, with a metadata key of its own. A later registration that finds that root resolving into the directory drops the appended row, wipes its regenerable caches and answers the row that stores the root. Since v0.104.0 the drop keeps the three directories under the dropped key and logs a warning that names their paths ("automatic row drop retained unsaved sidecar paths", 958 to 964). The design document states the cost: "unsaved recovery records and drafts remain under the dropped key and are not automatically carried to the surviving row", and says that "a row its user registered on purpose beside a slow relinked root that later resolves into it is dropped the same way".

How it came to be so (`dev/v0104-team/reports/decisions-Lead104.md`, the entry of 2026-10-08T09:58:35Z): the review found that the appended-row drop wiped the sidecar's `Drafts`, `drafts-trash` and the already landed `editor-sessions`, and the lead ruled that no automatic path removes unsaved sidecar state, the drop "leaving those three directories in place with a warning (carrying them to the surviving row welcome, not required)", while the user's forget, unregister and Everything reset remove them, as the owner had ruled for a reset and a forget. The owner was told, not asked: the survey on a draft's links carried the rule as information with "Say if you want that path to remove them too" (`dev/v0104-team/evidence/Lead104/surveys/draft-links.body.md`, its last paragraph), and the owner's answer chose among the link options and said nothing of it (the entry of 2026-10-08T14:37:46Z in the same decisions file). The lead held it for a later home (`dev/v0104-team/reports/held-observations-Lead104.md`, row 10 of the index) and disposed it as "Drafts record and owner notice: automatic row drop keeps unsaved state" (`dev/v0104-team/reports/held-observation-dispositions-Lead104.md`, row 10).

What is pinned, by tests of the `chan-workspace` suite and not by a run of the product: the test `a_row_appended_beside_an_unanswered_probe_is_dropped_once_its_root_answers` (`library.rs` 2796, compiled for unix only) drops an appended row that holds a seeded recovery record, a draft and a discarded draft and finds the record, the draft's text and the trash directory on disk afterwards; and `sweep_orphans_preserves_keys_with_unsaved_sidecars` (2193) shows the orphan sweep leaving alone a key that no row holds when it contains any of the three directories (`sweep_orphans_in`, 1498 to 1504).

A reading of the released tree made for this item, of two places and not run: after the drop the dropped key belongs to no row, and the sweep that reclaims keys without a row skips a key that holds any of the three directories; by a search of the tree for `sweep_orphans`, that sweep is called by its own tests alone. No wider search was made for something that removes or re-homes the retained directories.

Not established: that the drop has happened in use to a row holding drafts or recovery records. Whether a user can see, reach or remove the retained directories afterwards through any surface: the lead's ruling names forget, unregister and an Everything reset as what removes this state, the design document describes an unregister as finding a row by its root and wiping that row's key, and the dropped key has no row; that was not traced in code for this item. How much such state accumulates. The pin that shows the retention is compiled for unix only, and what the version's CI ran on macOS or Windows was not read for this item.

## Desired contract

This item asks for a decision, the owner's: whether unsaved sidecar state under a row that chan drops by itself is kept with no end, as now, or has an end. The record names three choices: keep it under the dropped key with the warning, which is the present rule; carry it to the surviving row, which the lead's ruling calls welcome and not required; or let the automatic path remove it, which the survey's body offered.

## What to do

Put the question to the owner with the three choices and what each costs. Two things a seat can prepare first. A reading at source of what a user has after a drop: whether the Drafts group or any other surface lists drafts under a key that no row holds, and whether a forget, an unregister or an Everything reset reaches that key. And a constructed drop that shows it: a draft and a recovery record made under an appended row, looked for from the surviving row after the drop. If carrying is chosen, a design comes first: the two rows have different metadata keys and the existing pin itself seeds a recovery record under each of them, so a carry has collisions of name and path to answer.

## Boundaries

`crates/chan-workspace/src/library.rs` (the appended-row drop, the orphan sweep, the explicit unregister and reset), `crates/chan-workspace/src/draft_store.rs` where a carry would read or write, their tests, and `crates/chan-workspace/design.md`; a user-facing surface only if the decision asks for one. Not changed: the owner's rule for explicit actions (a State reset keeps drafts, drafts-trash and recovery records; an Everything reset and a forget remove them); what the drop wipes of regenerable caches; the conditions under which a drop stands down (a live handle of the row, a held writer lock, a claim); an existing in-workspace `.Drafts/` or `attachments/`, which stay ordinary user files.

## Acceptance

1. The owner's decision is recorded here with the choice named.
2. A written reading, with a constructed drop run in a guest, of what a user can see, reach and remove of the retained state after the drop: from the surviving row, and by each removal the lead's ruling names.
3. If the present rule is kept: the design document says what, if anything, ends the retained state, and a test fails when that sentence is untrue.
4. If the state is carried: a draft and a recovery record made under the appended row are listed and open from the surviving row after the drop, a collision of name or path is answered as designed, and no byte is lost; pinned red first.
5. If the automatic path removes it: the drop removes the three directories, pinned red first, and the changelog says that an automatic drop discards the unsaved drafts and recovery records under the dropped row.
6. For any change: fmt, clippy and the whole `chan-workspace` suite green at the commit in the owning guest, in the ordinary arm and in the symlinked-temp arm, since the range resolves and compares paths.
