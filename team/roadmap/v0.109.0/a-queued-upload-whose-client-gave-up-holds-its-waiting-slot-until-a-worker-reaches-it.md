# A queued upload whose client gave up holds its waiting slot until a worker reaches it

Status: built on 2026-10-21 on the seat's branch; the section Built 2026-10-21 holds the evidence; it closes at the GA as shipped.

Record before the release: carried into v0.108.0 at the v0.107.0 GA on 2026-10-17, still raised for a decision.

Record before the release: raised on 2026-10-16 by the lead from the runtime seat's design of the check 62 item, read at source by that seat and reproduced in its guest; written down with the repair's shape; built by the runtime seat after its lane's items; owner the runtime seat.

## What was seen

Read at source at `a54966060` and reproduced (`dev/v0107-team/evidence/Runtime107/c62err/`). An upload (`POST /api/fs/upload` through `stream_upload_tracked`, `routes/files.rs`) that arrives while the transfer lane's two active slots are held (`bulk_transfer.rs` `ACTIVE_CAPACITY`) waits in the lane's queue with its request open. When its client closes the connection, the feed sees the body fail at once, but the handler waits for the job's outcome, and the job reports only when a worker reaches it, that is when an active slot frees. In check 62's queue legs that is about 70 s under the throttle: the queued, cancelled upload's answer and its log line arrive beside the first held upload's completion. Meanwhile the abandoned request holds its place in the queue: every upload behind it is announced one place further back, it counts toward `WAITING_CAPACITY`, and its own answer comes late. One boundary of the reading: the feed sends the body through an eight-message channel, so a queued upload whose body fills that channel stops reading and does not see its client's close until a worker drains it; the item covers the queued upload whose feed is still reading when the client closes.

Not established: a reading in a served instance under real traffic; the queue depth at which a user notices.

## Desired contract

A queued upload whose client has gone leaves the queue at once: its handler answers and its job is dropped without a worker's turn, so the next queued upload moves up and no slot's time is spent on a request nobody waits for.

## What to do

When the feed records a body failure while the job is still queued, the handler returns at once with the check 62 item's answer (400 `upload_incomplete`, one INFO line) and removes the job through one lane call that removes it only while it is still queued, under the lane's lock, so the removal cannot race a promotion (a job promoted first runs to its own end as an active job does); an active job keeps the check 62 item's behaviour (the handler waits for the job's own end so the atomic writer's discard and the slot release stay the lane's). The lane's timing for active uploads is not changed.

## Boundaries

The transfer protocol, the lane's capacity and its cancel for active jobs are not changed; the check 62 item's answer and log line are reused, not redefined; the terminal and standalone Files lanes share the function and take the same change.

## Acceptance

1. Red first: two uploads holding the slots with open bodies, a third queued and then closed by its client (its body small enough that the feed is still reading when the client closes), a fourth queued behind it: at the tip the third's answer comes only when a slot frees and the fourth is announced at the position behind it; with the repair the third answers at once with 400 `upload_incomplete`, the fourth's announced position moves up by one, and when the next slot frees the fourth is promoted first, asserted by the order of the answers and the announced positions, not by latency (one mutant: the early return removed, red on the fourth's position).
2. The queued job leaves no partial file and releases nothing it did not hold (the lane's counters read before and after).
3. The whole `chan-server` suite green at two threads at the landed tip; check 62's queue legs run under Hygiene107's edited assertion.

## Lead's rulings 2026-10-21

(U5) the cancel reaches uploads in the three lanes (workspace, standalone Files, terminal), the row's subject; the route answers a download's or a copy's pair as not cancellable here, with one named status, since those leave the queue on a client close already. (U6) An explicit cancel of an active upload is a new first-wins reason beside the lane's cancelled and stalled states, written by the cancel route's lane call and mapped by the completion to its own outcome and to the 400 `upload_incomplete` answer; the shutdown's and the panic's 503 stay as they are, with a forced-order pin (a cancel after a shutdown stays a shutdown) and a mutant. (U7) The scoping is the routing match the tracking keys already give: the cancel names both keys and matches only the transfer started under the same pair, and the route's authority row equals the upload routes' (owner and grantee); no verified-subject claim is added. (U8) The page both calls the carrier and aborts its request; the route answers an unknown or finished pair idempotently with one named status and no error line; the registry entry is removed when the handler ends on every path; the design pins the cancel-before-registration order and the handler-ended order beside the queued and active cases.

- Q1 (a cancel before registration has nothing to signal, and a request whose bytes already fill the feed's channel when the job is admitted may keep its slot after the client's abort): the design's bounded experiment opens the build, as a Fabler diagnosis before a fix: in the runtime guest, with no product change, a constructed run that forces the cancel before registration with a body over the channel's capacity and the client's abort, beside D2 (the whole body read, hyper's EOF drop) as the positive control, each bounded, its outcome reported to the lead the same day. Pre-ruled on the two outcomes: if the buffered order keeps the slot, the repair is registration at the handler's entry, before the destination parsing, under the same two keys (so the carrier finds every started request) together with the queued job's feed racing its send against the cancellation capability (so a blocked feed is released by the cancel), and no tombstone cache (a protocol policy the owner has not asked for); if the order withdraws the request by itself, the pin records that order and no boundary is added. The code task carries the pin either way.

- Q2 (the cooperative cancel can be chosen at delivery while the writer has published): the finalization boundary the design recommends: a compare-exchange taken before the upload's streaming callback returns; a cancel that wins first discards; a finalization that wins first makes the carrier answer `not_cancellable` and the upload's own request keeps the writer's actual success or error, never a 400 beside a committed target; one forced-order pin on each side of the boundary with a mutant each; no deletion or rollback of a published target. Best-effort cancellation through finalization is refused: a late failure report beside a stored result is the mtout class of defect.

- U9 (code task): a two-file pin, one request after another under the same pair, so the registry guard's removal at the handler's return is held, not only at the body's end.

- U10 (settled before the server half lands): a duplicated tab copies `sessionStorage`, and a `?w=` URL opened twice or the `default` fallback gives two pages the same `xfer-<w>-<n>`, which the registry's 409 would refuse on the second page's upload, an upload that works today. Ruled: the page mints `xfer-<w>-<clientNonce()>-<n>`; Frontend109 makes that change with its pin as one small commit of its own on its branch, the whole of that change, landed before the server half (the lead integrates the prerequisite first); the registry's 409 stays as a guard. It is added to Frontend109's task 4 as its first item.

- U11 (page contract, Frontend109's upslot page half): on local HTTP/1.1 the carrier shares the browser's six-connection pool with the uploads, so the page aborts its request first and then sends the carrier without awaiting it.

- U12 (page contract, before mtout's page half): a cancel that lost (`not_cancellable` after finalization, or `not_found`) is the page's only news, since the page aborted its own request; the page reports "finished before the cancel" and refreshes the listing, never a bare "Cancelled" beside a landed file. Added to task 4's mtout page design item.

- U13 (code task): Q2's finalizing state is named in `is_cancelled`, `check_cancelled`, `is_stalled`, the completion's `Done` arm (not the `_ => Cancelled` arm) and the drop; the capability returns whether its compare-exchange won; pins: finalization then a client close keeps `Done`, finalization then a shutdown keeps `Done`; under Q1's first outcome a cancel before `submit` sets the live entry's own flag, which is not a tombstone.

- Note taken into the item: the desktop's native uploads send no tracking keys, so the carrier cannot reach them and their cancel stays the abort alone.
