# The live record's Unix corroboration reads a held flock and a live pid, not the pid holding the flock

Status: built on 2026-10-22 on the seat's branch; the section Built 2026-10-22 holds the evidence; it closes at the GA as shipped.

Record before the build: carried into v0.109.0 at the v0.108.0 GA on 2026-10-21, still raised for a decision.

Record before the release: raised at the v0.107.0 cut on 2026-10-17 by the lead from a finding outside that version's items, recorded in the round's decisions as the ledger's entry; written down with the repair's shape; owner by lane at the next version's launch.

## What was seen

On Unix, `live_record`'s corroboration of a devserver record reads two facts, that some process holds the record's flock and that the recorded pid is alive, and takes their conjunction as the recorded pid holding the flock. A pid reused by another process inside the window between the holder's exit and the record's read satisfies both facts while the holder is gone (the review seat's note at the restart item's design, 2026-10-16). Not established: that the window, of microseconds, is met in use. On Linux `/proc/locks` names a flock's holder by pid.

## Desired contract

A live record is corroborated by the pid that holds its lock where the platform can name it; elsewhere the record states what its two facts establish and what they do not.

## What to do

The corroboration reads which pid holds the flock where the platform can say (Linux: `/proc/locks` for the lock file's inode), and keeps the two-fact reading elsewhere with the design text saying so.

## Acceptance

1. A Linux reading through `/proc/locks` with a pin that constructs the reused-pid case (a holder exited, another process at its pid, the record read), red first.
2. The other platforms' two-fact reading stated in the design text with its window.

## Lead's rulings 2026-10-22

L1: the fallback to the two-fact reading stands for an unreadable or absent `/proc/locks`, an unavailable anchor and no matching held flock, recorded at debug level with its reason, since a refusal would break live detection and bounded stop for real holders; the match key is the identity the kernel prints for the lock file, read from the prober's own momentary OFD read lock in `/proc/self/fdinfo`, not `stat`'s, so a btrfs lock file is matched. L2: a malformed or ambiguous matching record is an unavailable reading and takes the fallback with its reason; no convenient pid is ever chosen. L3: the record-rewrite construction with the positive control on the guest's btrfs disk and on tmpfs satisfies acceptance 1; natural pid reuse was not forced.

## Built 2026-10-22

`chan-workspace::daemon_lock::corroborate_daemon_record` returns the verdict and a typed method or reason. Its shared lock probe retains the opened description across a contended `FileLock::try_exclusive`; a free probe explicitly releases its flock and never takes an OFD anchor. The Linux module takes a nonblocking one-byte OFD read lock, obtains one distinct kernel-printed device/inode key from its own fdinfo and explicitly releases the anchor. The non-Linux and Windows-specific pins are present but not executed in this Linux guest. The lead owns the combined suite and Windows target check.

With one file per churn thread, 810168 table reads contained 20782 missing and 267948 duplicated target rows; a second immediate read recovered 150075 of these but remained unavailable 138655 times. Saved anomalous tables, holder fdinfo and a contended second descriptor establish that the target stayed locked. This identifies a mechanism at source, not the unobserved exact error variant of the original integration call or a guarantee under arbitrary contention.

The Linux lookup retries once when its first complete table has no matching flock or has ambiguous matching rows. Both reads use the same kernel key. A usable first owner or another error is final; an unavailable second result retains its reason and the live two-fact fallback required by L1/L2. The design text states the retry's limit: concurrent proc iteration is not an atomic snapshot. Acquisition, owner comparison, record format, signalling and platform fallback remain as specified. Sustained churn can still take the fallback, so this is bounded recovery from one transient reading and not elimination of that contract's wrong-pid window.
