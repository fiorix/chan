# A window's tabs sit below the title bar on Windows

Status: raised by the owner on 2026-11-08 with the code on `feat/titlebar-tabs-windows` (`a97778380`), six commits on `feat/titlebar-tabs`; accepted for v0.126.0; built by the owner, under the lead's review, built and tested in the Windows VM before the owner's acceptance over VNC.

## What is there now

A chan-desktop window on Windows is a decorated window: the system draws its title bar with the title, minimize, maximize and close above the page, and every pane draws its own tab strip below it (`web/packages/workspace-app/src/components/Pane.svelte:1423`), so the title bar's row holds nothing of the page and a window with stacked panes spends that row and one strip per pane on chrome. While a window's Computers screen shows, the desktop attaches the launcher's menu bar to that window (`desktop/src-tauri/src/launcher_panel.rs:564-576`). Lines cited at `fa08475b3`. The companion macOS row puts a window's tabs in its title bar on macOS only: its page asks for the title bar where the installed app advertises what that host needs, and on Windows it does not ask.

## Desired contract

1. On Windows, Settings > Global > Title bar tabs (the companion row's preference, off by default, live in every window) is offered where the installed app advertises both `set_titlebar_tabs` and `titlebar_window_control`. A page served to an app without both never asks, so no window loses its frame to an app that cannot drive its controls.
2. On, the window turns frameless and its page draws the window tab bar in the band the title bar held: the pane map button and the active pane's strip, as on macOS, and at the right minimize, maximize or restore, and close, three buttons of 46 DIP each (the caption button width Windows 11 draws) at the bar's full height, in the Windows 11 look, close turning red under the pointer, out of the tab order. The band is 32 DIP at 100 percent, the height Windows 11 gives its title bar and caption buttons, and the tab row's height when a page zoom makes the row the taller, so no empty strip sits under the tabs. The window keeps the system's shadow, border and rounded corners; it resizes from every edge, the top included; it snaps when dragged to a screen edge and with the Win+arrow chords; minimize and maximize animate; a maximized window fills its monitor's work area and leaves the taskbar in view. Turning the mode on or off does not move the window or change its size.
3. Minimize and maximize act as the system's buttons do, and the maximize button shows restore while the window is maximized, following a maximize or restore made any other way. Close is the close request the system's button and Alt+F4 make, so chan's Hide, Close and Cancel prompt and the rest of the close handling run. A press on a control keeps the keyboard where it is and never drags the window. The bar's empty stretch and the gaps between tabs drag the window, and a double-click there maximizes or restores it.
4. The buttons keep their 46 DIP through a page zoom and a change of display scale; a window in the mode keeps its size across hide and restore, maximized included; fullscreen draws no controls, and leaving it brings them back.
5. While the Computers screen shows over a window in the mode, the bar hides its button and strip and draws at its left the launcher menubar's File, Edit and View as text buttons in the look of a Windows 11 menubar, each opening its native menu under the button, then the window's title, all clear of the controls and taking no press elsewhere, so the band still drags the window; the title follows a rename. Edit's items act on the launcher, and Ctrl+Shift+T, Ctrl+Alt+W and Ctrl+Q act there once each, as the menubar's accelerators do where the menubar is attached.
6. Turning the setting off gives every window its frame and title back at the same size, and a window showing its Computers screen its native menu bar.

## Boundaries

- Linux and FreeBSD are unchanged; macOS is the companion row, `a-windows-tabs-sit-below-the-title-bar-on-macos.md`.
- The page decides: the desktop never makes a window frameless on its own, and a page load from another origin than the page that turned the mode on gives the window its frame back before the new page lays out.
- `titlebar_window_control` and `titlebar_menu_popup` act on the invoking window only; with the companion row's four commands they reach local and gateway-served windows alike.
- Stated limits: hovering the drawn maximize button opens no Snap Layouts menu, since Windows 11 shows it only for a native hit test answering `HTMAXBUTTON` over the button and the page's webview covers that area; Win+Z and dragging to a screen edge still snap. The Computers screen draws no Window menu, since it lists the windows itself, so Ctrl+Shift+N is not served there. The drawn menus open on a press and do not switch on hover or with Left and Right as a native menu bar does.
