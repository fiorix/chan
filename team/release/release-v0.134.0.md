# Release v0.134.0

Shipped 2026-11-24 from `0.134.0-ga`: the release commit `chore(release): 0.134.0` (every version pin moved from 0.133.0 to 0.134.0, with the three lockfiles regenerated, both Nix hashes and the Fedora specs, and these documents folded in: the CHANGELOG section, this record, the release index line, the withdrawn row moved to `done/` and the roadmap's completed entry; its sha is `351c60181` and its own proofs are under Validation) on the integration branch `v0134/int` over its content tip `d06e95fbc`. No release candidate was cut: asked on 2026-11-24 whether to cut a candidate or to release, the owner answered "ga", and the release goes from the integration branch, as v0.126.0 to v0.133.0 did.

The cycle opened on 2026-11-20 with the seventeen rows v0.133.0 left and three rows new from that cycle. On 2026-11-22 the owner named `fix/fetch-update-deprecation` (`f5657caab`) the baseline. On 2026-11-23 the owner took the Ghostty-only plan: xterm.js leaves chan, with the `terminal.ghostty` key retired under a warning period. A four-seat team built that plan's first milestones in one day, four rows were added (the xterm.js removal, the key's later rejection, a held key on macOS and two inherited stream findings), and the mouse-mode prelude row was deferred, measured and withdrawn. The owner wrapped the team up at 18:13Z the same day ("Land in-flight work, then wrap up"), and at 23:36Z set the release: "let's get ready to release chan with dev/unitcomment-wt merged onto v0134/ghostty-ng-cs-tunnel-wg and both onto v0134/int", "all other roadmap items will move to next version". Four minutes later the owner asked for the team's unmerged unit too ("id like all ghostty work merged"), and on 2026-11-24 took in the tunnel driver recovery from his Mac ("in"), a review before the integration branch moved ("yes") and every Ghostty finding the reviews left ("i want all fixes related to ghostty to be in this version", "including this copy takes the match fix"). Twenty-four rows passed through v0.134.0: none closes as shipped, three ship work and carry the rest, one closes withdrawn, and twenty moved to v0.135.0 with nothing of theirs shipping.

`v0134/int` was fast-forwarded on origin from `f5657caab` to `d06e95fbc` on 2026-11-24: 45 first-parent commits, 24 of them `--no-ff` merges, and 252 commits that are not merges, over 360 files (+28,018 and -14,354 lines), 316 of them outside `team/` and `CHANGELOG.md` (+27,721 and -14,252). ## What shipped

No row closes as shipped in v0.134.0. Three rows ship work and carry the rest in `team/roadmap/v0.135.0/`, where each row's status line and dated sections hold its contract, builds and readings in full; the entries below give the change and its deciding readings. Evidence paths in this record are under `dev/ghostty-web-ng-team/evidence/` in the development tree unless they say otherwise.

- `noxterm`, Ghostty is the only terminal engine a user can choose. Every new, restored and reconnected terminal opens on Ghostty, and Settings, the terminal's body menu, help, the docs and the manual no longer offer an engine. The `terminal.ghostty` key is retired under a warning period that starts with this release: a stored `false` in `server.toml` warns once per process that the key has no effect and will be rejected later, a stored `true` is silent, loading never rewrites the file and the next save drops the key. The preferences API accepts a boolean `ghostty` and ignores it, a non-boolean refused with 422. `chan config set server.terminal.ghostty` succeeds as a no-op with a warning on stderr, `get` prints `true` with the same warning, listings omit the key, and every spawn exports `CHAN_TERMINAL=ghostty`. A tab left open across the upgrade keeps the xterm.js of its own bundle until it reloads. Unit tests at each boundary with their mutants, and browser check 341 (set, get, the listing and the warning count) red at `f5657caab`; its refusal arm, corrected to read an exact 422 and the server's logged rejection, passes in a guest. Not shipped from this row: xterm.js is still in the bundle, and a failed Ghostty load still falls back to an unlabelled xterm.js terminal. That fallback ships with little mounted coverage: the search addon and its 1,000-match cap, the mounted paste path, the PRIMARY selection state machine, the WebGL context-loss path and the scrollback copy through the serialize addon have no mounted test left. The key's later rejection is the row `rejectkey`, which waits for the warning period.
- From the same unit, `chan config` returns a failed write to stdout as an error and exits 1, where it panicked with exit 101. A refused-stdout case covers `set` and the four `get` arms.
- `ghosttyng`, what a user sees change in a terminal. The row height comes from the widget's own measurement at a 1.2 line height. Box and block glyphs are drawn per cell, and there is one overlay scrollbar. On the light theme dim text is held at a contrast of 4.5 to 1, as xterm.js held it. A program that tracks motion gets one report per cell, with code 35 for motion with no button down, where chan sent 32, and Alt in the modifier bits. Under mouse tracking a reported press, drag or release clears a stale local selection unless a local selection gesture is in progress, and a hover report never does. A find match no longer replaces the selection: the menu's Copy takes the selection, else the match while the find bar is open, else the scrollback, and the copy chord takes the selection only. In a browser tab, a chord chan declines from a focused terminal is the browser's, as from any other surface: Ctrl+Shift+A in a control terminal opens Chrome's tab search.
- `ghosttyng`, pointer handling on the widget. Selection, mouse reports, link presses and scrollbar drags capture the active pointer, ignore other pointers while it is held, recover a gesture whose capture was lost silently, end once on release, cancel or loss, and release a reported button the program saw pressed. A reported press ends with a release only while the program still tracks the mouse; once tracking is off, held state clears silently. Pen, touch and the scrollbar thumb follow the same rules. The app stops intercepting the strip and the find bar, cancels the Linux desktop's middle press for PRIMARY paste, and leaves a reported double click to the program. The widget gains a synchronous `Terminal.getCursor()`. `make web-check` and the 68-check browser suite in the guest on the merge's tree (`gwng-12`), five of six pointer checks red at the base as predicted (`gwng-13`), and the hover report reading 32 without the change and 35 with it (`gwng-19`). Not read: WebKitGTK and the Mac.
- `ghosttyng`, focus. The widget focuses synchronously, with `preventScroll` and no retry, and every late focus the app makes (after the loads, a seeded prompt, a menu's clipboard read or write, the inspector's close) asks who holds the keyboard when it runs. In the guest, on two paths of checks 345 and 346, a seeded terminal in an unfocused pane and a delayed menu paste after a pane switch sent a focus report to the program without the change and kept the keyboard with it (`gwng-23`). Check 344, the reload case, never reached its legs that day; it is repaired below.
- `ghosttyng`, selection, copy, highlights, paint, theme, scrollback and find on the widget, and the app's move onto them. The widget carries selection and copy, named highlights, ordered paint layers, grid and render options, a read-only core facade, `setTheme` with a contrast floor, scrollback as a byte budget and an all-match find. `TerminalTab.svelte` constructs the widget with `copy`, `joinDrawnWraps`, the 1.2 line height, cell-drawn box glyphs and the overlay scrollbar; the app's `terminal/ghosttyCompat.ts` and `terminal/ghosttySelection.ts` are gone; find paints its active match on the `search-active` highlight layer; a theme switch goes through `setTheme`; secret masks are painted from the widget's `mask` paint layer. The two private reads the reviews found (the theme adapter's `buildWasmConfig`, the masker's replaced `render`) went with that, and the browser and pixel instruments read the widget through its public API. Two limits, by reading at the tip: the app still passes scrollback as a line count, not the byte budget, and still runs its own `GhosttyFind` over the core facade, so nothing calls the widget's find. The app's terminal tests moved from a stand-in terminal onto the real widget: 11 test files still name the xterm stand-in, down from 40.
- `ghosttyng`, the fixes taken in on the owner's word, each with a test read red before it. A public `getRangeText` so the menu's Copy takes the find match; the widget's own find keeping its matches and count across writes, and a step no longer restarting from the newest match; a drag resting within 30 px of an edge keeping its end under the pointer; a failing paint layer no longer forcing repaints or making `write()` throw; `select()` mapping its rows through the viewport when history exists; pointer focus that does not scroll the page; a refit when the device pixel ratio changes; the light theme's contrast floor on the widget, with a `contrastBackdrop` so the floor holds in a window built transparent, which is every desktop window; a chord the terminal lets out keeping its default; a focus report after mode 1004 went off and on unheard; the keyboard staying with a form field the user moved to during a load; a middle press making its pane the active one on the Linux desktop; the inspector's close returning the keyboard to a shown composer; a wake asking who owns the keyboard before it blurs; and the link menu's late focus gated like any waited focus. ## Retrospective

Highlights: a day after the owner decided it, a user can no longer choose xterm.js, with every browser check that asked for it ported and the app's mounted terminal tests on the real widget in place of a stand-in. The runner defect v0.133.0's record named, an exit 0 over a red, was closed in six reviewed rounds, with planted reds read at every layer. The cumulative read of the stream and wake changes that v0.133.0 lacked was made first, and two of its findings are now a row. The mouse-mode prelude, carried since v0.132.0, was closed by a measurement of the core and not by a build. In the integration, a rerun of four failed checks alone separated check 345's profile leak from a regression before anything was blamed on the tree; a mounted probe showed that the review's ordered focus fix broke Split right, and the rule was narrowed before it merged; and the fix order that could not make the contrast floor reach the desktop said so, which is how the limit was closed with a widget option the same morning.

Lowlights: the team was wrapped up before its last unit was gated. That unit reached the release as a branch nobody had reviewed, with a type error already at its tip, two patches that no longer applied and 21 test files never run; no test had run on any of the three merged trees when they were made, and the first two web checks were red. No milestone was accepted as a whole, so nothing reached `v0134/int` in steps and the whole range landed as one fast-forward after one session's integration. The integrator's first statement of what was unreviewed was wrong. Checks 344 and 345 were written without a browser: 344 had never reached a leg in any recorded run, and the team's recorded cause for it, a 409, was not the cause. The contrast floor fix as first delivered did nothing in the desktop, the owner's main surface. Three tests that could not fail had passed every web check until a read found them. No engine but Chrome read any of it, no full gate ran on the line, and both pushes skipped the hook. No row closes: eighteen of the rows the cycle inherited moved on to v0.135.0 with nothing done, the Windows and AppImage readings among them. AUR `chan-desktop-bin` went red on `archive.archlinux.org` again, on 2026-11-23 and on the first dispatch of 2026-11-24.

Honest feedback: this is a terminal release in which the engine every terminal now runs on changed its row height, its glyphs, its scrollbar, its pointer and focus handling, its selection, its copy and its theme path in one range, and every statement about it rests on Chrome in a guest and on jsdom. The owner's Mac runs WKWebView and the Linux desktop runs WebKitGTK; neither was read, and the pixel harness was not run at the new geometry. The tunnel fixes ship on tests behind seams: the `cs tunnel` route has no real gateway reading, and the driver recovery answers a diagnosis whose leading candidate was never confirmed on the Mac. The xterm.js fallback is still the path a failed load takes, and it now has less mounted coverage than it had a release ago. What worked was the discipline around the volume: a red test per finding, hand-backs kept verbatim, reviewers that did not build the range, and limits reported in the words of what was not read. What the cycle lacked was an acceptance step at each milestone. A team that builds faster than its units are gated leaves the gating to whoever integrates, and here that was one session on the night of the cut.
