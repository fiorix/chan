# Release v0.128.0

Shipped 2026-11-12 from `0.128.0-ga`: the release commit (the pins moved from `0.127.0` to 0.128.0 in the eleven manifests, the ten of v0.127.0 and the new `web/packages/ghostty-web-ng/package.json`, the three lockfiles regenerated, both Nix hashes harvested, the Fedora specs, the rows moved to `done/`, the CHANGELOG section, the release index line and this record) on the round's integration branch `v0128/int` over its final tip `891489b6c`, which holds the milestone 3 candidate `d00ca051e` and one documents commit after it, `891489b6c`, correcting the manual's WireGuard text to the desktop that ships (one tunnel per node, no client file). No release candidate was cut: the owner released from the integration branch, as v0.126.0 and v0.127.0 did, on his word in the team chat on 2026-11-12 ("let's carry on and release, ga and tag").

The release opened on 2026-11-09 with the owner's plan to fork the parts of ghostty-web chan uses into a widget of its own, taken in parts over several releases with part 1 in this one, and the same day he added a bound on the find frame's feed at the largest scrollback, the CLI's local devserver calls going through a proxy, a theme switch that leaves a ghostty terminal's colours, wrapped copies that paste broken, opening files in chan from the system, and keeping a find's match across a resize, and moved the per-node WireGuard ingress and the system CA store here from v0.127.0 (the tmux title bar, accepted the same day, was built the next and moved to v0.127.0). On 2026-11-10 he added the pane tree's reading of what a terminal runs, the tests still red under the heaviest load, the tmux card and New Window in a devserver's tmux window, took four rows from v0.127.0's second reading (the silent launch, the Windows handoff pipe, the minimized window, the unused-token test) and two from the fixes of his Mac checks (a desktop upload's cancel and error paths, a moved tmux window), collected that reading's remaining findings in one row when he closed v0.127.0, and had the WireGuard ingress's gateway lane built with an agent outside the round. On 2026-11-11 he added the Dashboard slides' centering, `make clean` and an end-to-end check of the basic flows at the round's start, accepted the seven rows the round's own checks and readings drafted ("All seven into v0.128.0"), and handed in two changes his Mac agent built from the lead's orders, the window title back in the title bar and the macOS wake blank. Thirty-two rows: thirty shipped, one shipped in part with its later parts carried, and one carried unstarted.

The round ran as a team from 2026-11-11 00:47Z to the candidate's last CI reading at 2026-11-12 04:13Z: the lead and three worker seats from the start (the terminal and web lane, the desktop lane, and the core, gateway and WireGuard lane) and a fourth worker seat, for packaging and leftovers, added that evening. Each worker took one task at a time from a written order with its base sha, files and acceptance evidence, in its own worktree, and handed back commits or a hashed patch with its reds, mutations and gate; the lead's subagents ran the independent reviews, the guest readings, the gate kit and the small fixes; the lead read every hand-back against its order, merged, gated, pushed and dispatched CI, and put every open question to the owner in a survey. The owner built three of the release's changes outside the seats (the WireGuard gateway lane with an agent, the title bar and the wake repaint with his Mac agent), ran four batches of Mac checks at one sha, and answered the surveys he was at the keyboard for.

## Retrospective

Highlights: thirty-two rows through one round of about 27 hours, every hand-back read against its order and every larger one by a reviewer with no anchor in the seat's report, and those reviews caught three defects their own gates could not show: the find feed's learned read cost halving towards zero on every cheap feed, which a constant-chunk measurement cannot see and a mixed-load leg now proves; the WireGuard desktop stage's e2e oracle matching a start-log line the desktop never emits, with 29 helper tests and 19 mutations green against a fixture written in the same wrong format, fixed in a nine-item round with a fixture copied from a real guest log; and the Finder open queue left stuck by a panic in its drain, so that every later open would have queued until a restart, fixed by a hold whose drop hands the queue on to a new task. Reading before code paid on Windows: the VM reading of the handoff pipe found an elevated process of one account reaching another account's desktop, which no reading of the source had raised. The WebKit arm found two Linux faults the morning it was built, and both rows were accepted, fixed, merged and green in the arm within hours. The WireGuard row's acceptance 4 ran against a real gateway stack in a guest with three nodes and both mixed-version directions.

Lowlights: CI found faults every guest had passed. The AppImage launched `chan-edit` because the bundler put three desktop files at the AppDir root and AppRun takes the first in readdir order, which the gate guest's btrfs gave in creation order and CI's ext4 runner in hash order; the opener tests expected `/var` where the macOS runner resolves `/private/var`; and earlier in the round, Linux-only drop code that was dead on macOS and Windows, a socket test that took frame timing for wire order on macOS, and a descriptor compared as text under CI's built-in Administrator. Int gate 3 took three runs: one red at rustdoc, which no merge gate ran until then, and one red because the lead ran two merge gates beside two seat gates during the gate's web check, breaking the one-heavy-job rule the lead had set after milestone 1, when the int gate, the PostgreSQL trials and the browser suite overlapped, the host swapped (zram at 12.9 G) and the suite lost three checks to the environment. The open-with contract survey waited about 90 minutes for a chan window while the owner was away, and its retry unit ran its first attempts without the control socket's environment; the stage merged ahead of the answer so the gate and the suites did not wait. The lead's control run of the 177 and 62 repairs tested the base checks, because the guest's bound source was the scratch worktree at an older sha, and was read as a red and withdrawn; and one followup file was overwritten by a `cat >` and restored from the session transcript. Six surveys timed out with the owner away.

Honest feedback: the macOS statements of this release rest on four batches the owner ran at one sha, `811bd5eb8`; what only a Mac can show after it (the open-with row's Finder path, the system CA on macOS, the WireGuard lines on the macOS desktop) ships unread, and the two rows the owner built were read on macOS by him alone.
