What you can do

  • Reach your devservers, and devservers shared with you, from Chan Desktop or any browser, without ssh.
  • Sign in once in your browser for the whole account.
  • Share a devserver you own with another account, and revoke it.
  • Reach a private gateway through WireGuard, inside Chan Desktop, with no VPN client.

What a gateway is

A gateway is a set of services that someone, your team or you, hosts on their own domain; chan is not an online service. Devservers dial out to it, and it publishes each one behind sign-in with GitHub, Google, or GitLab. To host one, read the gateway guide.

Account page tabWhat it holds
ProfileYour username, which is part of every devserver address you publish. You get four renames, and each disconnects your devservers, so pick one before you share.
TokensAccess tokens. Each devserver you publish dials in with its own.
DevserversMy devservers and Shared with me, each with Open and Copy link. The tab shows once the operator turns it on for your account.

Add the gateway in Chan Desktop

  1. Open the Gateways screen

    On the Computers screen press g, or click its title. g flips back.

  2. Fill in the Add gateway card

    FieldWhat to enter
    Gateway URLThe address the operator gave you, such as https://gw.example.com. It cannot be changed later; remove the gateway and add it again instead.
    Name (optional)A label for the card. It defaults to the URL's host.

    Then choose Add gateway.

    Click the card's Connect control, or press o, and sign in.

Sign in

Connect opens your browser on the gateway. Sign in with your provider, then answer Authorize an app on this computer? with Authorize to return to Chan Desktop. One sign-in covers the whole account.

The first time you connect a devserver shared with you, Chan Desktop asks Grant native access? Native access lets its windows read and write your clipboard, read files you select, save downloads, control Chan windows, and open links in your browser. Grant it only if you trust the owner; Revoke native access on its card takes it back.

Open a devserver

FromHow
Chan DesktopOn the Computers screen, the gateway's devservers are marked "via" the gateway. Connect one like any devserver; its windows open as native windows. There is no connect script and no control terminal.
Any browserSign in at the gateway's address, open the Devservers tab, and click Open. The browser lands on the devserver's own address, such as https://alice--3f9c2a7b1e04.p1.proxy.example.com/, on its Computers screen.

That address carries no token: your sign-in is the key. Copy link copies it, and it opens for the owner and everyone the devserver is shared with.

To list a devserver of your own, start it with a token from the Tokens tab; the Devservers tab shows the command. See Publish a devserver.

Share a devserver

Sharing gives another account the whole devserver, as much as a shell account on the machine. Share only with people you would give one.

  1. Add their email

    In Devservers, click Share on the devserver, enter their email, and click Add.

  2. They sign in

    The share reads pending sign-in until they sign in with that verified email, then active. They add your gateway to Chan Desktop, or open a link you copied for them.

Revoke on the share takes access away and ends their sessions on that devserver. The operator's part is in the gateway guide.

Reach it through WireGuard

Chan Desktop can reach a gateway inside WireGuard, running the tunnel in its own process: no VPN client, network interface, or root. Edit the gateway with the pencil on its card:

The operatorYou do
Enrolls keysChoose Generate WireGuard key, save, and connect the gateway, turning it off first if it is on. The Authorize page shows the key and the gateway's WireGuard nodes.
Hands you a WireGuard fileSet WireGuard file to its path, absolute or starting with ~/, and chmod 600 it. It takes effect when you save.

Once you authorize, the card reads enrolled and lists each node with its tunnel's state. Enrolled means:

  • The gateway holds your key on every node it lists. The desktop runs a tunnel to each, carrying only that node's devservers.
  • The key lasts as long as the sign-in that enrolled it. When that sign-in's token is revoked or expires, or the account is blocked, the key leaves every node.
  • At each connect the desktop follows the gateway's node list, with no new sign-in.
Details and limits
  • A card that reads not enrolled: connect to sign in has a key the gateway has not taken yet. Generating a key again replaces it, and the next sign-in enrolls the new one.
  • Generate WireGuard key refuses when the gateway does not offer enrollment.
  • The desktop keeps the private key in a file of its own; only the public key leaves the computer.
  • Setting or changing the WireGuard file replaces an enrollment.
  • Sign-in runs in your system browser, outside the tunnel, so the browser needs its own route to the gateway's address.
  • With a WireGuard file, the desktop's requests, window feed, and webviews reach the file's AllowedIPs through the tunnel; other destinations connect directly.
  • A WireGuard file has one [Interface] with PrivateKey, Address, and optional MTU, and one [Peer] with PublicKey, Endpoint, AllowedIPs, and optional PresharedKey and PersistentKeepalive.
  • A file with DNS, Table, FwMark, ListenPort, a hook such as PostUp, SaveConfig, an unknown key, or a second peer is refused with the reason. On Unix, so is a file the group or others can read; on Windows, restrict it yourself.
  • To re-read a file edited in place, clear the field, save, and set it again.
  • A devserver's machine needs no open port, DNS name, or certificate to be published: it dials out.
  • To remove a gateway, select it in Select mode and choose Remove. The native access you granted its shared devservers goes with it.
  • chan devserver ls lists a gateway's devservers, marked; manage them from the Computers and Gateways screens, not with chan devserver connect.
  • Tools that need your desktop, such as cs tunnel, work through a gateway connection too, on the devserver owner's desktop.
  • A row under Shared with me does not say whether the devserver is online; while it is offline, its link answers not found.
  • gw.chan.app is the maintainer's own experimental deployment, with sign-in off by default. You cannot sign up there.
