What you can do

  • Split a pane right or down, and move between panes from the keyboard.
  • Keep two sets of tabs in one pane, on sides A and B, and flip between them.
  • Drag a tab to another pane, to a pane's edge to split it, or to another window of the same workspace.
  • Jump to any pane, side, or tab from the pane map.
  • See how the window's connection is doing, and whether typing in a terminal got through.
  • Rearrange a whole window with Hybrid Nav, then keep the result or throw it away.
  • Hide a window with its terminals still running, or close it.

Panes, tabs, and sides

A window holds panes, and a pane holds tabs: terminals, editors, file browsers, and other apps. Every pane has two sides, A and B, each with its own tabs. The A/B button in the pane's top bar flips between them, and the pane menu beside it opens new tabs, Hybrid Nav, and Close pane.

When a tab label has keyboard focus, press Enter or Space to select it and focus its pane. Activating the label with assistive technology selects the tab; its close button is a separate control and becomes visible when reached with the keyboard, even on an inactive tab.

The Browser column is chan in a web browser; Same there means the desktop's key for your system.

Tabs in the title bar

On the macOS and Windows desktop, the active pane's tabs sit in the window's title bar, and the other panes draw no tab strip. Turn this off in Settings → Global → Title bar tabs.

  • The pane map button, at the left of the bar, lists every pane, its sides, and their tabs. Type to filter by tab name, then pick a tab with the arrows and Enter, or click it.
  • Cmd+Shift+A on macOS, Ctrl+Shift+A elsewhere, opens the same list in any window, a browser's included.
  • A terminal's row also shows what runs in it, such as bash or vim (see what the pane map shows for a terminal).

Connection health

A window keeps asking the chan server for replies and shows when they come late or stop.

With tabs in the title bar, an indicator sits at the bar's right end: always on a window of another library, such as a devserver's, and on a local window only while the connection is in trouble. Otherwise it shows in the status bar at the window's top right, and only in trouble.

The indicator readsWhen
Bars aloneReplies come back in time. They show faded before the first reply.
A time, such as 850 ms or 2.4 sSlow: a reply has waited 1 s, or two in a row took 400 ms or more. Two in a row under 300 ms make it healthy again; while they recover it shows no time.
stalled 6 sStalled: a reply has waited 4 s or more with nothing arriving meanwhile.
reconnectingThe window's connection dropped. It reads this until a new connection answers.

Click the indicator, except while the cover is up, for how long the state has lasted, such as Slow for 12 s, a chart of recent replies with the missed ones marked, the latest, median and worst reply times, and the time of the last contact.

A terminal tab watches its own connection too, and a note at its top right says what that means for your typing.

The terminal's note saysWhen
No response for 6 s. Waiting for the connection.A check has gone 4 s with no reply and nothing else arriving.
Connection lost. Typing is not being sent until it reconnects.The terminal's connection closed. The note stays until it is back.
Reconnected. Input typed while disconnected may not have been sent.You typed while the connection was down or silent. It shows for 12 s, and typing hides it once it has shown for 4 s.

When the window's connection drops, a cover reading reconnecting to the chan server greys the window and takes clicks and keys, with the attempt count and the time since the drop. The window keeps redialling, and the cover lifts on its own once the server answers.

Details and limits
  • The window asks every 3 s while it is in front and has had a reply, and every 20 s otherwise.
  • A title bar with tabs stays above the cover, unless a dialog or an overlay holds the keyboard, and shows only the window's title and the indicator, with the window's own buttons.
  • The cover shows once the connection's status has held for 600 ms, about a second after a connection closes, when the first redial, at 500 ms, does not get through.
  • A connection that goes silent instead is dropped after 15 s on a window reached over a network that is in front and has had a reply, and after 45 s otherwise.
  • On waking, or when a hidden window shows again, the window checks its connection and drops one that does not answer within 3 s, or up to 10 s on a slow link. After a drop for silence, by either deadline or the wake check, the cover waits until 45 s after the last contact.
  • Reached over a network means another library in Chan Desktop, or a browser page whose address is not a loopback one such as localhost or 127.0.0.1, so a page reached through an ssh forward counts as local.
  • In Chan Desktop, a window of another library, such as a devserver's, adds Restart connection and Abandon to the cover once the drop has lasted 8 s (see Disconnect and reconnect).
  • A terminal's notes show only on the tab in front, and not under the cover.
  • A stalled terminal connects again on its own once the window's connection has been healthy for 3 s, at most once every 30 s. One you typed into during the stall waits until its connection times out instead.
  • A terminal checks its connection when it opens and every 20 s, and 1 s after you type, at most every 2 s, so a stall shows soon after you type.
  • In the status bar, the close button beside the indicator hides it until the connection reaches a state it has not shown since it was last healthy.

Hybrid Nav

Hybrid Nav arranges a window from the keyboard. Press Cmd+. on macOS or Ctrl+. elsewhere, or pick Hybrid Nav from the pane menu or the command launcher. Changes show in place as a draft: Enter keeps them, and Esc throws them away.

KeyWhat it does

Details and limits
  • With a terminal focused, the split, pane, and tab keys above still reach chan. Ctrl+/, Ctrl+[, and Ctrl+] stay with the shell.
  • Ctrl+D also closes a tab that is not a terminal, on every system. In a terminal it is the shell's end of input; on a terminal whose program has ended it closes the tab.
  • In an empty pane, Close tab and Close window close the pane first. If the pane's other side still holds tabs, the pane flips to them and the A/B button flashes instead.
  • On the desktop, closing the last tab of a standalone terminal window closes the window.
  • Only a tab from the same window splits a pane at its edge; a tab from another window joins the pane as a tab.
  • Tabs move between windows of one workspace, or between standalone terminal windows of one machine, and never between the two kinds.
  • A tmux window's panes have no sides and take no dropped tabs (see tmux control mode).
  • With tabs in the title bar, the bar also shows the window's title, such as ↓ local / chan Window 1, or ↑ build-host / chan Window 1 for a devserver's window. It sits beside the pane map button while the window has no tabs, and left of the A/B button once one opens.
  • As tabs fill the bar, the title shrinks to about two tabs' width before the tabs start to scroll. A cut title ends in an ellipsis, shows whole as a tooltip, and scrolls to its end while you point at it.
  • Privacy mode shows only the generated name, such as Window 2. A tmux window shows its title at the right end and a tmux badge in place of the A/B button.
  • On Windows the bar draws minimize, maximize, and close at its right end.
  • When a tab out of view reports a status such as done or blocked, the pane map button shows the most urgent one (see program status).
  • The close prompt starts on Cancel, and Esc cancels. If the desktop refuses a hide or a close, the prompt stays up and says why.
  • The Hybrid Nav key list shows only the keys this window can use, and each key in it is also a button.
  • If another window or client changes the layout while Hybrid Nav is open, the draft goes stale, and only Esc and h answer.
  • In a tmux window, Hybrid Nav previews splits and sizes and asks tmux for them on Enter. It stages no tabs and swaps no panes there.

Windows and the Computers screen

Reload remembers the pane you just focused. During an unusually rapid burst of layout changes, the address can stop updating for about ten seconds; reloading during that pause can restore the last saved pane.

The Computers screen lists the local machine, your devservers, and every window on them, shown or hidden. It has a page of its own.

In the desktop app, a window list's Focus, Show, or Open action restores a minimized window and brings it forward. The Window menu and cs window open do the same. Choosing About while its window is minimized restores that window. A new standalone terminal window opened by New Terminal, New Window, or a second launch with no window to raise comes forward once it is built.

The command launcher

Press Cmd+K on the macOS desktop, or Ctrl+Alt+K on Windows, Linux, and web, for one searchable list of every command, setting, and window. It has a page of its own.
