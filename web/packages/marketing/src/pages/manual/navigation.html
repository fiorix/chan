What you can do

  • Split a pane right or down, and move between panes from the keyboard.
  • Keep two sets of tabs in one pane, on sides A and B, and flip between them.
  • Drag a tab to another pane, to a pane's edge to split it, or to another window of the same workspace.
  • Jump to any pane, side, or tab from the pane map.
  • Rearrange a whole window with Hybrid Nav, then keep the result or throw it away.
  • Hide a window with its terminals still running, or close it.

Panes, tabs, and sides

A window holds panes, and a pane holds tabs: terminals, editors, file browsers, and other apps. Every pane has two sides, A and B, each with its own tabs. The A/B button in the pane's top bar flips between them, and the pane menu beside it opens new tabs, Hybrid Nav, and Close pane.

The Browser column is chan in a web browser; Same there means the desktop's key for your system.

Tabs in the title bar

On the macOS and Windows desktop, the active pane's tabs sit in the window's title bar, and the other panes draw no tab strip. Turn this off in Settings → Global → Title bar tabs.

  • The pane map button, at the left of the bar, lists every pane, its sides, and their tabs. Type to filter by tab name, then pick a tab with the arrows and Enter, or click it.
  • Cmd+Shift+A on macOS, Ctrl+Shift+A elsewhere, opens the same list in any window, a browser's included.
  • A terminal's row also shows what runs in it, such as bash or vim (see what the pane map shows for a terminal).

Hybrid Nav

Hybrid Nav arranges a window from the keyboard. Press Cmd+. on macOS or Ctrl+. elsewhere, or pick Hybrid Nav from the pane menu or the command launcher. Changes show in place as a draft: Enter keeps them, and Esc throws them away.

Details and limits
  • With a terminal focused, the split, pane, and tab keys above still reach chan. Ctrl+/, Ctrl+[, and Ctrl+] stay with the shell.
  • Ctrl+D also closes a tab that is not a terminal, on every system. In a terminal it is the shell's end of input; on a terminal whose program has ended it closes the tab.
  • In an empty pane, Close tab and Close window close the pane first. If the pane's other side still holds tabs, the pane flips to them and the A/B button flashes instead.
  • On the desktop, closing the last tab of a standalone terminal window closes the window.
  • Only a tab from the same window splits a pane at its edge; a tab from another window joins the pane as a tab.
  • Tabs move between windows of one workspace, or between standalone terminal windows of one machine, and never between the two kinds.
  • A tmux window's panes have no sides and take no dropped tabs (see tmux control mode).
  • With tabs in the title bar, the bar also shows the window's title, such as ↓ local / chan Window 1, or ↑ geekom / chan Window 1 for a devserver's window. It sits beside the pane map button while the window has no tabs, and left of the A/B button once one opens.
  • As tabs fill the bar, the title shrinks to about two tabs' width before the tabs start to scroll. A cut title ends in an ellipsis, shows whole as a tooltip, and scrolls to its end while you point at it.
  • Privacy mode shows only the generated name, such as Window 2. A tmux window shows its title at the right end and a tmux badge in place of the A/B button.
  • On Windows the bar draws minimize, maximize, and close at its right end.
  • When a tab out of view reports a status such as done or blocked, the pane map button shows the most urgent one (see program status).
  • The close prompt starts on Cancel, and Esc cancels. If the desktop refuses a hide or a close, the prompt stays up and says why.
  • The Hybrid Nav key list shows only the keys this window can use, and each key in it is also a button.
  • If another window or client changes the layout while Hybrid Nav is open, the draft goes stale, and only Esc and h answer.
  • In a tmux window, Hybrid Nav previews splits and sizes and asks tmux for them on Enter. It stages no tabs and swaps no panes there.

Windows and the Computers screen

The Computers screen lists this machine, your devservers, and every window on them, shown or hidden. It has a page of its own.

The command launcher

Press Cmd+K on the macOS desktop, or Ctrl+Alt+K on Windows, Linux, and web, for one searchable list of every command, setting, and window. It has a page of its own.
