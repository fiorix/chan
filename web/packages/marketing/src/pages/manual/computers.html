What you can do

  • See every window on the local machine and your devservers, shown or hidden, in one place.
  • Open a standalone terminal, a new workspace, or another window of a workspace.
  • Turn workspaces on and off, and connect or disconnect devservers.
  • See how the connection to each devserver is doing, down to its reply times.
  • Hide a window while its terminals keep running, and show it again later.
  • Do all of it from the keyboard, one letter per action.

Show, hide and focus windows

Each window row has two buttons: focus brings the window to the front, and the eye shows or hides it. A row reads Terminal Window 2 or Window 1, and This window marks the window the screen is over.

Hide a window to get it out of the way while its work runs: start a build or an agent, hide its window, and check on it later. Close it when you are done with it.

Connection health

Chan Desktop checks the connection to each devserver it is connected to, and marks how it is doing beside the devserver's name on its card.

Beside the nameWhen
Bars aloneReplies come back in time. They show faded before the first reply.
A time, such as 850 ms or 2.4 sSlow: a reply has waited 1 s, the last one took 1 s or more, or the last two took 400 ms or more. It shows the longer of the last reply and a wait of 1 s or more. Two replies in a row under 300 ms make it healthy again; while they recover it shows the bars alone, still in the warning colour.
stalled 8 sStalled: a reply has waited 4 s or more with nothing arriving from the devserver in the last 4 s.
reconnecting 20 sThe connection dropped, or stayed silent until Chan Desktop dropped it, 60 to 90 s into the silence. It counts from the drop, in minutes from 2 minutes on, until a new one answers.

The card in front adds the detail under the devserver's address: a chart across the card of the last 60 checks with a red mark for each missed one, and under it, on one line, the latest, median and worst reply times and, as Missed, how many of those checks got no reply; its tooltip gives the count against the checks in the chart and the time of the last contact. On a narrow card the line wraps, Missed first. The mark beside the name says how the connection is doing, with its count where it has one.

Details and limits
  • While a connected devserver does not answer, its card lists none of its windows and workspaces and reads Windows and workspaces show again when the connection recovers. They come back once it answers. A window that loses its connection meanwhile offers Show in Computers on its cover, which brings this card to the front (see a window's connection health).
  • Chan Desktop checks each devserver once, however many of its windows are open: every 3 s while a Chan Desktop window has the focus, every 20 s otherwise, and once more when Chan Desktop gains the focus and when the Computers screen shows.
  • The check crosses everything between the two machines, such as an ssh tunnel or a gateway and its tunnel, so a devserver reached through a gateway shows the same marks.
  • A sleep of the local machine does not read as slow or stalled: a check in flight across a sleep is not timed, and neither is a reply that took longer than 45 s. On Windows, a sleep under 45 s across a connection's very first check can show once as a slow reply, which the next two replies clear.
  • No mark shows on This machine's card, on a devserver that is connecting or disconnected, or in a browser. A devserver that runs a chan too old to answer the check shows none either, except the reconnecting count while its connection is down.
  • The mark and a window's cover watch different connections. A window can be covered while its devserver reads healthy, as when the window's own connection or its workspace is gone, and a devserver can read stalled while a window still gets data.
  • Privacy mode leaves the mark, its words and the detail shown: they say how the connection is doing and name no machine.

Gateways

Press g, or click the screen's title, to flip to Gateways: the same carousel for the gateways you connect through, with Add gateway last. The devservers a gateway gives you show on Computers as cards that read via and the gateway's name.

Connect to a gatewayAdd a self-hosted gateway, sign in, and open the devservers it publishes.
Details and limits
  • On macOS, Cmd+P with no Chan Desktop window focused opens a standalone terminal instead.
  • On Windows and Linux the window's menu bar shows only while the screen is open.
  • The letters leave forms, a window name you are editing, dialogs and the command launcher alone. Shift or uppercase letters work the same; Cmd, Ctrl and Alt combinations keep their usual meaning.
  • Click a window's name to add your own text; the row then reads Terminal Window 2 [build].
  • A devserver's icon shows its state: connected, connecting, connection lost, or disconnected. The mark beside a connected devserver's name shows how its connection is doing: see Connection health.
  • Select, in the top bar, puts a check on each workspace, devserver and tmux connection. The bar at the bottom turns the checked ones on or off, or removes them after you confirm.
  • The top bar's command button opens the command launcher with the Computers scope alone.
  • In a browser the cards stand one under another, a window row has Open window and the eye, and the letters do not apply. Closing a browser tab ends its window's terminals; hide the window first.
  • When several browser pages show one devserver's screen, only the session leader shows and hides windows. See Collaboration.
