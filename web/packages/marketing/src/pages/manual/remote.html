What you can do

  • Keep terminals, agents and workspaces running on another machine while your laptop sleeps or moves.
  • Open them as native windows from Chan Desktop, or as tabs in any browser.
  • Reach the machine over ssh, Eternal Terminal, a LAN address, or a Lima or WSL VM on your own computer.
  • Forward ports, move files and share the clipboard between the two machines.
  • Publish devservers through a gateway, and share them with other accounts.

Which route

A remote chan is always a devserver. The route is how this machine reaches it; each route's name links to its setup.

No chan on the remote machine? tmux -CC over ssh opens a tmux server's windows as Chan windows: see tmux control mode.

What you need

  • There: chan, running a devserver. It listens on 127.0.0.1:8787 until you bind it elsewhere. See Run a devserver.
  • Here: Chan Desktop for native windows, or any browser.
  • Between them: one route from the table.

"There" can be this computer too: a Lima or WSL VM, a container, or a second devserver with a chan home of its own.

Whatever the route, the devserver holds the terminals, windows and workspaces. Disconnecting closes the windows here and leaves everything running there. Several clients can use one devserver at once: see Collaboration.
